# ADR-037: A turn declares what it answers on the message it is already posting

## Status

**Accepted (2026-09-15), amended twice on 2026-09-16.** This record settles which mechanism is authoritative for "which message does this answer", states the determinism property the design is tested against, and records the options that were rejected and why.

**Three pieces of code are BUILT and deployed to dev on 2026-10-27; NOT VERIFIED on a deployment.** `placeholderContent` in `backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3575` puts the declaration on the placeholder CREATE, verified by `backend/test/lib/a-placeholder-names-what-it-answers.test.ts`. The `paired_by` promotion in `backfillFromUpdateEvents` corrects a provenance label once the declaration arrives, verified by `backend/test/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival-backfill.test.ts`. The sweep deferral in `createExchangesFromDatabase` waits for that declaration rather than racing it, verified by `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-sweep-waits-for-the-answer-it-is-pairing.test.ts`, with migration `053-a-sweep-waits-for-the-answer-it-is-pairing.sql` recording the two column contracts it moves. Nothing here is measured on a deployment.

**The second amendment of 2026-09-16 corrects this record against the code rather than adding to it.** The matrix under "What is true today" said that on a default deployment a bypass entry, a battle side, the alt, a handover, the stop evaluation and the task-answer repair all declare nothing. That is false, and the reason is in the same file this record already cites: `placeholderContent` reads `knownInboundMessageId` with no turn-queue gate. The matrix is rewritten below around the mechanism rather than around a list of paths, because a path list is what went stale.

**Its sibling is ADR-038.** That record takes the other half of the same question: this one is about whether a turn declares and how confidently the label says so, and ADR-038 is about whether the id a turn declares from a late content search is the right one. Its option F is built, and a reader who takes this record's `declared` label at face value without ADR-038's bound has half the picture.

## Problem, and who has it

An operator reads one headline number to decide whether the assistant is responsive: time to first feedback, averaged over the turns the platform can measure. That average is taken over pairings, and a pairing is the platform's answer to "which user message does this assistant message answer".

On 2026-09-15 about 45 percent of the turns that had a ledger TTFF were excluded from that average, and they were disproportionately the slow ones. The population matters as much as the share: the denominator is turns with a ledger TTFF, not turns, so the figure says nothing about turns the ledger never measured at all. The exclusion is correct on its own terms: `backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/analytics-query.ts:3185` keeps a pairing labeled `swept` out of the TTFF population because a swept pairing is a bounded search for a plausible reply, and its latency is the gap to whatever that search chose. What was wrong is which turns carried the label.

The mechanism, as found: Kinesis archival batches on a five second window (`backend/lib/stacks/archive-stack-aurora.ts:868-869`), the only declaration of what a turn answers rode the closing UPDATE, and a turn whose first feedback takes longer than five seconds cannot have its user message and its placeholder in one batch. The cross-batch sweep therefore minted the pairing from the placeholder alone, at a moment when every carrier of the declaration was still NULL, and labeled it `swept`. The slower the turn, the more certain that outcome. A measurement that silently censors its own worst cases is worse than no measurement, because it is on the dashboard.

Both halves of that mechanism are addressed by what this record decides: the declaration now also rides the placeholder CREATE where the turn holds the id, and the sweep now waits rather than minting from a record that is not finished. Neither is deployed, so the 45 percent reading above is the last measurement anyone has.

This is for the operator reading the Latency tab, and for the developer extending the message path who needs to know which of the several things in the tree that look like a pairing key actually is one.

**The 45 percent figure has a source, and it is one paragraph rather than an impression.** It was recorded as a dated measurement in `docs/guides/developer/LATENCY-TARGETS.md`, which the guide's git history holds; the guide keeps the reading, without the dated figures, under "Reading a TTFF step". It was measured on the development deployment through the signed path, as the second half of a pair: on 2026-09-11 no turn with a ledger TTFF was excluded, and on 2026-09-15 about 45 percent of them were, with the `basic` tier reporting no TTFF at all for two days. The two readings apply the same predicate to the same question before and after the `paired_by` label existed to answer it, which is what makes the second one evidence rather than an anecdote.

What it is still not is a figure this repository can reproduce on demand: no `queryType` returns it, so a reader who wants it today asks the cluster. And it is a share of the turns the ledger measured, never of turns. Those two qualifiers travel with the number everywhere it is used below, because the failure this record spends its length on is a measurement that did not say what it counted.

## What is true today

Thirteen mechanisms in the tree answer some part of "which message does this answer". They are listed with what each is authoritative for, when it runs, and what it costs.

The first eleven are the ones a reader of the archive meets: carriers archival can read, the pairers that read them, and the ledger that borrows their result. The last two bind a message to a turn somewhere else entirely, and they are in the table because leaving them out is what let an earlier draft of this record claim a complete inventory that was not one. They decide nothing about `exchanges`, and that is the distinction, not their absence.

### The one authority, and the one thing it is not

**The turn's own declaration is the authority**, and mechanisms 3, 4 and 5 are three carriers of it rather than three facts. `transformToMessageRecord` folds all of them into `messages.answers_message_id` at one point (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:846`), and migration 042 exists so that both pairers read one column instead of each reaching into the carrier it happens to understand. Every downstream reader resolves the carriers in the same order, column before JSON, in one lateral per query.

**Both pairers read the UPDATE row first and the CREATE row second**, which is why putting the declaration on the CREATE needs no query change. The in-batch pairer takes `updateRecord?.answers_message_id || botRecord.answers_message_id` (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1246`) and the sweep takes the same two in the same order inside a `COALESCE` (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1586`). The order is right for the reason it was written, which is that a placeholder is created before the turn knows what it answered. It is also right for the new case, and for the opposite reason: where the CREATE carries the declaration and no update row exists yet, the `COALESCE` falls through to the CREATE and reads it, which is what lets the sweep's deferral resolve on the first pass instead of waiting a window.

### Which turns declare, stated as the mechanism rather than as a list of paths

An earlier draft of this record answered this with a table of entry kinds and got it wrong, so the rule is stated first and the table is derived from it. The rule is three questions asked in order, and the turn queue flag governs only the second.

**1. Does the router hold the inbound id at the moment it composes the placeholder? Then the CREATE declares, with no flag involved.** The router keeps one id, `knownInboundMessageId` (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3520`), and `placeholderContent` declares from it or declares nothing (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3575-3578`). There is no turn-queue gate on that composition, and a duel side's placeholder declares from the same id through its own composer (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:4876` and `:4950`). The id reaches that variable two ways and only two: a bypass caller declares it as the `AE.message.id` request attribute, which `bypassToLexEvent` sets from the caller's `userMessageId` (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:1091`); or the lazy `findInboundMessage` lookup resolves it for something else this turn needed, a task row or a queue entry, and assigns into the same variable on the way past (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3531`).

**2. Does the id reach the PROCESSOR? Only with the turn queue on, and that is the whole of what the flag decides here.** `holderRecordFields` puts `userMessageId` on the processor event (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:4145`) under `owesHolderRecord`, which requires `turnQueueEnabled()` (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:4141`); the flag is off unless `-c turnQueue=true` (`backend/lib/stacks/agent-classification-common.ts:400-405`). In the processor, `recordWhatThisTurnIsAnswering` (`backend/lambda/src/assistant-async-processor.ts:634`) is the only other writer of that field and runs only under `event.rememberHolder` (`backend/lambda/src/assistant-async-processor.ts:852`), which is the same gate from the other side. Both processor-side carriers read exactly that field: the answer's Metadata (`backend/lambda/src/lib/async-processor-core.ts:4515`) and the close-out (`backend/lambda/src/lib/async-processor-core.ts:4192-4193`, `backend/lambda/src/assistant-async-processor.ts:2824-2825`). So the flag decides whether the UPDATE declares as well, never whether the CREATE does.

**3. Is the turn answered from the handler as a `DIRECT` quick response? Then it declares on its one message, unconditionally, and buys the read if it has to.** A greeting or acknowledgment posts once and has no later event to correct itself on, so it takes the bypass id where there is one and otherwise pays the memoized channel read (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3919`) before declaring on its own content (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3940-3943`). It is the one path in the system that buys the id, the owner priced that on 2026-09-11, and a lookup that finds nothing still posts the marker without inventing a target.

**THE RESIDUAL, AND IT IS THE HEADLINE.** An ordinary Amazon Lex turn that is not answered `DIRECT`, opens no task and does not queue answers none of the three questions. Nothing hands it an id, nothing makes it pay for one, and on a default deployment it declares nothing on either of its message events. It pairs `in_batch` where its user message and its placeholder land in one five second archival batch, and `swept` otherwise, and `swept` is excluded from the headline time to first feedback by name. That is one population and it is the only one. Everything the earlier matrix listed beside it holds an exact id and declares on its placeholder today.

| Does the turn declare? | Default deployment (queue off) | Turn queue on |
|---|---|---|
| A bypass entry (`@all`, a battle side, the alt, a handover, the stop evaluation with a caller-held id, a task-answer repair) | Yes, on the CREATE, from the id the caller declared. No flag involved | Yes, on the CREATE and again on the UPDATE |
| An Amazon Lex turn that opens a task or queues | Yes, on the CREATE, from the id the lookup resolved for the task row or the queue entry | Yes, on the CREATE and again on the UPDATE |
| An Amazon Lex turn answered `DIRECT` from the handler | Yes, on its own content, buying the read where it has to | Same |
| The queue fold and the triage turn | Does not exist | Yes, through a different carrier: the answer stamps `answeredQueued` and the queued pass mints `declared` rows from it |
| **An ordinary Amazon Lex turn, none of the above** | **No, on either event. Inference is the only mechanism: `in_batch` or `swept`** | Yes, on the UPDATE, once the processor resolves the id |

**Two properties of that table are worth reading off it directly.** The flag moves one cell, the last one, and it moves it from "nothing" to "the UPDATE". And the declaration a turn makes on its CREATE is not the same KIND of fact in every row: two of them are an id a caller handed over, and two are an id a search produced. The label does not currently say which, which is the provenance question under "What is not yet true" and the reason ADR-038 exists.

**Which makes the placeholder declaration more load-bearing than the earlier draft allowed, for a different reason than it gave.** It is not the only declaration a default deployment has; it is the declaration that arrives on the row the sweep reads, at the moment it reads it, which is what lets the sweep's deferral resolve immediately for every row in the table's first three lines instead of waiting a window for an UPDATE.

### The two things that are not the authority, and cannot be made into it

**The correlation marker is not that authority and cannot become it by reading it differently.** It names the TURN, not the message. On the Amazon Lex entry it is `sha256(channelArn, senderArn, transcript, 90 second bucket)` truncated to 16 hex characters (`backend/lambda/src/lib/correlation.ts:83`), which is a one-way function of what the person wrote and not a handle on the message they wrote it in. ADR-022 establishes why: Amazon Chime SDK sends an Amazon Lex fulfillment exactly `CHIME.channel.arn`, `CHIME.sender.arn` and `x-amz-lex:channels:platform`, measured across fourteen days of conversation logs on all three classifications, and the message id is not among them. The only binding the channel flow records for a marker is `corr#<id>` to the PLACEHOLDER's id (ADR-022 section 2b), which is the other end of the pair.

**The ledger has no opinion about the target at all**, which is why it can never disagree with the pairing and why it also cannot repair it. `turn_events` keys a response on the correlation marker parsed from content; the person's own message carries no marker, so the writer records `user_message` with `turn_id` NULL and says so (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/turn-events-live.ts:289`). Migration 031 chose to borrow the exchange's `user_message_at` rather than invent a key in the writer, and records the borrowing as `anchor_source = 'exchange'`. So the ledger's anchor quality is exactly the pairing's quality, and the headline reads `anchor_source` before it trusts a ledger figure (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/analytics-query.ts:3185`).

## Is the set coherent or accreted

**Accreted in carriers, coherent in precedence, and with one real conflict that this record closes.**

The precedence rule is genuinely one rule and it is applied the same way in every reader: a declaration is obeyed, and inference runs only where no declaration exists. The in-batch pairer skips a bot record whose declaration names a different message and applies its three heuristics only under `if (!declaredTarget)` (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1291`); the sweep's `OR` arm requires the declaration to be NULL before any heuristic applies (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1596`); the promotion only ever moves a label upward and only on exact equality with the exchange's own user message (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1953`). Nothing in the set overwrites a declaration with a guess. Against tenet D7's "one source per question", the question "which message does this answer" has one source, and every other mechanism is a carrier of it, an inference where it is absent, or a diagnostic about its absence.

**Where two of them gave different answers for the same turn, and what closes it.** Not about the target: the two pairers apply the same three heuristics and the sweep's anti-join plus `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` means the first writer wins and the second is a no-op. The disagreement was about the CONFIDENCE LABEL, and the label is what the headline filters on. The same turn received `declared` or `swept` depending on whether the UPDATE carrying its declaration reached the archive before the sweep ran, which is a race, not a property of the turn. That was two answers to one question and it was the tenet D7 violation in the set. It is also why about 45 percent of the turns with a ledger TTFF fell out of the average: a label decided by timing selects on latency by construction.

**The deferral closes that conflict for every turn that has evidence coming**, because the sweep no longer answers before the record is complete. What it does not close is a turn nothing ever declares for: that turn takes `swept` on the merits, which is one answer rather than two, and it is a coverage gap rather than a tenet D7 violation.

**Two smaller frictions, both known and both currently held in place by a test rather than by a shared definition.**

The first is that "is this bot message response-anchored" is asked in **three classes of predicate**, and the count that matters is the class rather than the call site. They are the bounded extraction `correlationMarkerOf` (`backend/lambda/src/lib/correlation.ts:116`), the loose substring test the two pairers share deliberately so they cannot pair different sets (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1188`, and its `LIKE` twin at `backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1600`), and the full-pattern SQL `substring` the readers use (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/analytics-query.ts:3200`, `backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/turn-events-backfill.ts:65`). The bounded grammar has ONE definition, in TypeScript, and is repeated as a SQL literal in two reader files because a SQL string cannot import; `backend/test/correlation-id-is-readable.test.ts` pins every one of those copies to the same bound, and it checks that the one TypeScript reader that could import instead of copying does so. Three classes, one definition, and a guard over the copies is the accurate description. This record uses that framing throughout and no other.

The second is that **`paired_by = 'declared'` conflates three provenances of different strength**, which is recorded under "What is not yet true" below.

## The determinism property

One sentence, and the design now has it for every turn that finishes inside the staleness bound:

> **A pairing is deterministic when the component that knows which message is being answered writes that id onto a message event it is already posting, at the moment it posts it, so that no reader's result depends on when the reader looks.**

**It now holds inside the staleness bound, by construction, and it is convergent past it.** The property's second clause is the one the design used to fail: a reader's answer moved depending on which batch the UPDATE arrived in. The sweep's deferral removes that, because the pairing is not minted until the evidence is complete, so a turn whose UPDATE lands in batch N and the same turn whose UPDATE lands in batch N+3 receive the same `paired_by`. What remains is the expiry: a turn slower than the staleness bound pairs `swept` and is promoted when its declaration arrives, which is convergent rather than deterministic, and is deliberate because the alternative is a turn that never pairs at all.

**What guards it is the gate's shape, not a two-batch archival fixture.** `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-sweep-waits-for-the-answer-it-is-pairing.test.ts` pairs each disjunct of the deferral gate with the condition it encodes, evaluates the gate as the source actually writes it so a disjunct that is not in the file does not vote, asserts that the modeled disjuncts and the SQL's disjuncts are the same set, and proves it can fail by feeding the model the pre-change SQL and watching the in-flight turn be minted. `backend/test/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival-backfill.test.ts` pins the promotion's rules (only from `swept`, only on an exact id, never downward) and every one of its cases reads after the fold, so it still pins nothing about ordering. The two-batch archival fixture described in the next sentence would assert the property end to end rather than through the gate, and it does not exist: it drives the same turn twice, with the UPDATE arriving in batch N in one run and in batch N+3 in the other, and asserts the resulting `paired_by` is identical.

Three words in the property are doing work. *Knows* excludes anything that has to search for the id, and it is the word the ordinary Amazon Lex turn fails on, not the other two. *Already posting* excludes a new write, a new table and a new event, so the declaration cannot fail separately from the message it describes. *When the reader looks* is the clause the defect failed on, and it is the clause the deferral closes: the sweep and the in-batch pairer both read correctly and they read at different times, so the sweep now waits rather than answering from a record that is not finished.

Judged against it, mechanism by mechanism. The column reads the mechanism on its own terms; the deferral is what stops the sweep asking any of them before they can answer.

| Mechanism | Deterministic | What it races, if not |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The correlation marker | Yes, for what it answers | Nothing. It is derived from inputs a redelivery provably replays. It simply does not answer this question |
| 3. `answersMessageId` on the UPDATE | Yes, since the sweep waits for it | The fact is exact and its AVAILABILITY used to race the sweep, because the UPDATE is a later event than the CREATE the sweep reads. The sweep now defers until it lands or the turn is past the staleness bound |
| 4. The content declaration on the placeholder CREATE | Yes, where the id is known and was handed over | Nothing. It is on the row the sweep reads, at the moment it reads it. Where the id came from `findInboundMessage` instead, it inherits row 11's failure mode along with the id |
| 5. `closeOutAttribution` | Yes, on the same terms as 3 | It rides the same close-out, so the deferral reaches it the same way |
| 6. The in-batch pairer | No, on its inferred arm | The Kinesis batch boundary. Its own comment says the intervening-user test is weaker than the sweep's identical one because it holds only the records it was handed |
| 7. The sweep | Deterministic in WHEN it answers, still a search in WHAT it answers | It no longer mints before the evidence exists, so the label it writes stops depending on batch timing. Where nothing ever declares, it is still a bounded search whose latency is the gap to whatever the search chose, which is exactly why the headline excludes it |
| 8. The queued pass | Yes, in availability | Nothing in time. The reading produced one entry per queued message and the fold recorded which it rolled in. Its `declared` still comes from a model READING rather than from a handed-over id, which is a different question from determinism and is the third of the three provenances below |
| 9. The promotion | Convergent rather than deterministic, deliberately | It races nothing and never downgrades, so it converges. It is kept as the backstop the deferral's expiry hands work to: a turn past the staleness bound pairs `swept` and still has its declaration arrive later. Removing it would make the bound a censoring boundary of its own |
| 10. The ledger anchor | Inherits | It borrows the exchange's instant, so it is exactly as deterministic as the pairing it borrowed |
| 11. `findInboundMessage` | No, and it can be WRONG rather than merely weaker | A newest-first exact-content match over a bounded window. Repeated identical text binds to the newest, which under a queue is systematically not the message the running turn is answering. ADR-038 option F bounds the processor's late call by the turn's own start instant, which removes the population the queue creates; two identical sends before the turn began are still ambiguous, and the label still does not say the id came from a search |

### Why pairing does not simply use the correlation marker, and whether it could

This is the crux of the question, so it is answered in full rather than by pointing at ADR-022.

**The marker exists on the placeholder at post time, and it is the wrong identifier.** It labels the placeholder so the processor can find the message it must update, and it keys the dedup claim that collapses a retried fulfillment. Both jobs need an id that is the same for two attempts at one turn. Neither needs, and neither produces, a handle on the person's message.

**Nothing binds a marker to a user message, and the binding is not recoverable after the fact.** The derivation is a SHA-256 truncation, so it cannot be inverted. The one mapping the channel flow writes is `corr#<id>` to the placeholder's `MessageId`, taken as a duplicate guard.

**The user's own message cannot carry the marker.** ADR-022 section 3 settles this: the placeholder is a different message from the one the person sent, created later, so the label has to travel in the placeholder's own content. Writing a platform marker into a person's message would make the archive disagree with what they typed, and the platform already treats a marker in user content as attacker-supplied (`stripMessageMarkers`).

**Two entries already embed the message id in the marker, and using that would be a second grammar for a fact the same turn declares outright.** `mentionCorrelationId` produces `mention-<inbound MessageId>` (`backend/lambda/src/lib/correlation.ts:345`) and `arrivalTriageCorrelationId` produces `arrival-<MessageId>-<depth>` (`backend/lambda/src/lib/correlation.ts:217`). On those entries the marker genuinely is a deterministic pairing key, recoverable by parsing. It is not read that way, and should not be: those are precisely the entries where the channel flow already declares `AE.message.id`, so the turn already carries the fact on the carrier every reader uses. Parsing the marker instead would be two surfaces answering one question, and both are truncated at 64 characters (`backend/lambda/src/lib/correlation.ts:113`) while a `MessageId` may be 128, so the parsed form is lossy where the declared form is not.

**Could the marker be MADE the authority? Yes, and the cost is the reason it is not.** The channel flow holds a stable `MessageId` for every message and could compute `turnCorrelationId` over the same inputs the router will use, writing a `corr#<derived>` to `userMessageId` mapping before releasing the message. Four things are wrong with it. First, it is a conditional write on every inbound message at the flow, which ADR-022's Decision explicitly declined as both unnecessary and unsafe. Second, the derivation has to agree byte for byte with what Amazon Lex hands the router, and it does not reliably: the router normalizes the transcript before deriving (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:1849`), the flow strips markers and the battle command, a private address prefix is removed, and the 90 second bucket can straddle a boundary that `previousBucket` is not wired to catch (ADR-022 section 4). Third, every one of those mismatches is a SILENT miss that degrades to exactly today's sweep, so the new mechanism's failure mode is the old mechanism. Fourth, on the entries where the derivation is exact, the flow is already declaring the id outright and the lookup buys nothing.

So the marker cannot be the authority, and the reason is not an oversight one layer down. It is ADR-022's finding, which is that the Amazon Lex entry has no message id, surfacing in a second place.

## The tension this decision actually turns on

The question is not which mechanism is tidiest. It is that **on the ordinary Amazon Lex path the platform cannot know what a turn answers at placeholder time without paying a channel read inside the very metric the declaration exists to measure.**

Both halves of that are established rather than argued. The router has no message id, measured across fourteen days of Amazon Lex conversation logs on all three classifications and recorded at `backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3504`. Recovering it means `findInboundMessage`, which is a `ListChannelMessages`. And that read has been moved out of the router once already, on measurement: `recordWhatThisTurnIsAnswering` exists in the processor rather than the router because the router's copy "sat in front of the first frame" (`backend/lambda/src/assistant-async-processor.ts:619`), and the placeholder declaration declines to buy it for the same reason (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3562`).

So determinism and time to first feedback are in direct conflict on that path, and the conflict is not resolvable by choosing a better carrier. Every carrier in the tree is downstream of a fact nobody holds yet.

**That sentence is correct and it was read one step short.** "Not resolvable by choosing a better carrier" is a reason to stop choosing carriers, and the record went on to choose among six of them anyway. The fact nobody holds at placeholder time is held by update time, so the resolution is on the reader's side: the sweep waits for it. That is option G, and the first amendment at the end of this record is where the decision it should have reached is written down.

The design as decided on 2026-09-15 resolves the conflict by **declaring when the id is free and reconciling when it is not**. That is a compromise and this record calls it one, because the alternative reading, that it is simply the right design, would stop anyone looking for the thing that removes the compromise. The first amendment is what that looking found.

### Backstop or primary, judged honestly

Tenet D6 permits a reconciliation sweep behind an event path and says it must be a correctness backstop, never the primary mechanism. Applied to the populations separately:

The populations are the table above, and the judgment changed twice: once when this record found that a declaration on the UPDATE arrives after the row the sweep reads, and again when the sweep stopped reading at that moment.

- **A turn that declares on its CREATE, on any deployment.** The declaration is on the row the sweep reads, so the sweep either confirms it or is not needed. Backstop. Sound, subject to the provenance question below.
- **A turn that declares only on its UPDATE, which is any entry on a deployment with the turn queue on that did not already hold the id.** The declaration exists but on a later event than the CREATE. Before the deferral, the sweep minted first and the declaration arrived afterward to confirm it, which is tenet D6 inverted: the sweep doing the primary work and the event path doing the reconciling. With the deferral the sweep waits for that event, so the event path is primary again and the sweep is what runs when the event never comes. Backstop.
- **An ordinary Amazon Lex turn on a DEFAULT deployment.** Nothing declares on either event. The sweep is the only mechanism, and no deferral can change that: waiting for evidence that does not exist produces the same reading later. Primary, with no event path behind it, and this is the one population where tenet D6 is still not satisfied.
- **The queue fold, on a deployment with the queue on.** The reading declares, the queued pass mints from what it declared, and the sweep never sees that population. Backstop. Sound, subject to the provenance question below.

**So the boundary is neither the entry nor the flag; it is whether anything ever knew the id.** That is the honest judgment and it is the narrowest of the three this record has held. The deferral fixed the scheduling for every turn that has evidence coming. What is left is the turn that has none, and the only remedies for that one are buying a read (option E, rejected) or narrowing the Amazon Lex entry (option F, ADR-036's open measurement).

### What it would take to make it unconditionally deterministic

Four candidates were considered on 2026-09-15 and only one of those was available. A fifth, added by the first amendment, is available, is built, and is the one the decision now turns on; it is listed last because it is not a candidate of the same kind.

1. **Amazon Chime SDK carries the message id into the Amazon Lex request.** It does not. `CHIME.message.id` occurs zero times across fourteen days of logs on every classification. ADR-022 already names the arrival of this capability as a revisit condition. Not available, and not something this project can cause.
2. **The router pays the channel read.** Available and rejected twice on measurement. It would buy a label for the metric by degrading the quantity the metric reports, on every turn, to repair a subset of them.
3. **The channel flow dispatches the turn, so the id is in hand.** This is the only candidate that makes the question disappear rather than shrink, and it is ADR-036's options B and C. ADR-022 section 4 already shows the shape works: `@all` derives `mention-<inbound MessageId>` and declares `AE.message.id`, and those turns are deterministic today for exactly that reason. The blocker is not the pairing; it is that a channel flow cannot reliably keep Amazon Lex from being invoked on a message it releases, that `Target` is invisible to the flow so a private message has no in-flight route without Amazon Lex, and that ADR-036's own measurement of the Amazon Lex leg has not been taken.
4. **Keep reconciling, and make the residual visible.** What the design did before the fifth candidate, and what it still does past the staleness bound. On its own it is not determinism; it is convergence with a stated confidence label.
5. **Stop the sweep racing the fact.** Available, cheap, and indifferent to the entry: the sweep defers until the turn's update has landed or the turn is old enough that it never will. The other four are about WHERE the id is written; this one is about WHEN the reader asks. It is option G below, the first amendment at the end of this record is where it is decided, and it is built.

### What the residual window costs a reader of the metric

Stated in the terms an operator reads, because "eventually consistent" is not one of them.

- **The window the deferral replaced.** Before it, the turn was labeled `swept` from the sweep that minted the row until the batch carrying that turn's UPDATE, which is one batching window in the ordinary case and unbounded when the UPDATE is lost. The deferral removes that window for a turn whose evidence is coming: no row exists until it arrives, so no reader sees a label that is about to change.
- **What replaces it is a shorter and more honest one: the row is absent rather than mislabeled.** An analyst reading the most recent minutes sees a tail that is not populated yet where before it was populated wrongly. The exchange row is not only a pairing, so that tail is also missing `delivery_option`, `inbound_ms` and the ledger anchor that reaches the turn through it. This is the deferral's cost, it is real, and it is stated here rather than only in the amendment.
- **The deferral expires, and past the expiry the old behavior is the intended one.** A placeholder older than the staleness bound pairs on the evidence it has and takes `swept`, and a declaration that arrives afterward still promotes it. Without the expiry an abandoned or failed turn would leave analytics entirely, and a missing row is indistinguishable from a turn that never happened while a mislabeled one is at least counted.
- **A turn that never declares stays out permanently**, and on a default deployment that is the ordinary Amazon Lex turn described in the residual above. This is the part worth being blunt about: the exclusion is correct as a statement about evidence and it is a selection on latency, because the turns whose halves split across a batch are the slow ones. The deferral does not reach this population, because there is nothing for it to wait for.
- **And the promotion cannot reach it either, for a reason worth stating once.** The promotion's parameter is the update row's `answers_message_id`, which archival folds from `analytics.answersMessageId` or from a `<!--turn:-->` in the update's own content. With the turn queue off the first is never set, and the second cannot be: the update carries the model's answer, and the guardrail masks a `turn` marker in model output (`backend/lib/constructs/bedrock-guardrails.ts:68`). So the parameter is NULL, the `CASE` falls to its `ELSE`, and the label stands. For this population, on a default deployment, the sweep's verdict IS final, and that precondition belongs beside the claim every time it is made.
- **The reader cannot tell the two apart.** `ttff_reconstructed_count` (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/analytics-query.ts:2998`) counts "not in the average" without saying whether that is a window still open or a turn nothing will ever declare. Those call for opposite responses: wait, or go and fix the entry.
- **And a late declaration confirms a pairing without being able to correct one.** The promotion fires only where the declaration names the exchange's own user message. Where it names a different one, the code's own comment says that is "evidence the pairing is wrong" (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1946`) and correctly declines to invent a promotion from it. Nothing then counts it either, so the one signal that would prove the sweep guessed wrong is discarded silently. That is the single cheapest thing this design is missing.

## Options

**A. Declare on the placeholder CREATE, in content, where the turn already knows the id.** The declaration goes on the row the sweep reads, at the moment it reads it, so the race disappears for those turns. **The carrier can only be content.** Amazon Chime SDK materializes the placeholder from the Amazon Lex fulfillment return, and a fulfillment return carries messages and session attributes and no message metadata at all, so the placeholder has no `Metadata` field to write to. The 1024 byte cap that SPEC-MESSAGE-METADATA-CODEBOOK documents is therefore untouched and irrelevant here, which is worth saying plainly because it is the reflex objection to putting anything on a placeholder. What is available is `withTurnDeclaration` and the `<!--turn:-->` marker, which a `DIRECT` answer already uses for the same reason and which archival already folds to `messages.answers_message_id`. Cost: up to about 150 characters of a 4096 character content budget on a placeholder whose body is one short sentence, no new call, no new carrier, and no reader change.

Limit, and it is the real one: **only turns that already hold the inbound id declare anything**, which is a bypass entry, a handover, the stop path, and a turn that resolved the id for a task row or a holder record. An ordinary Amazon Lex turn still declares nothing on its CREATE, because the router still has no id to declare.

**The gain is larger than "a bypass entry already declared, now it declares earlier", and the table above is why.** `placeholderContent` reads `knownInboundMessageId` with no turn-queue gate (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3575-3578`), while every processor-side carrier is behind that gate. So on a default deployment this is not an earlier copy of a declaration that was going to arrive anyway: it is the ONLY declaration those entries get, on either event. With the deferral in place it is also what lets those turns pair IMMEDIATELY rather than waiting a window for an UPDATE, because the sweep's first disjunct is satisfied the moment the CREATE lands. It also makes the declaration depend on whether the lazy lookup resolved before the return is composed, which is deterministic per code path and subtle enough to be worth a reader's attention.

**And where the id came from the lookup rather than from a caller, the declaration is only as good as the lookup.** That is stated under option G and in "What is not yet true" rather than buried here, because a declaration minted from a search is not the turn's own knowledge and the label does not currently say so.

**B. Make the correlation marker the single authority and retire the adjacency paths.** Rejected, for the four costs in the section above. The short form is that it replaces a fact with a derivation, and the derivation's failure mode is silent and is the thing it was meant to replace.

**C. Carry the declaration out of band on the analytics record instead of in the message.** Rejected on the placeholder, for a reason that is specific rather than general. The out of band store is keyed by the message's own id (Technique B, SPEC-MESSAGE-METADATA-CODEBOOK; the write is at `backend/lambda/src/lib/async-processor-core.ts:4552`), and at placeholder CREATE time on the Amazon Lex entry that id does not exist, because Amazon Chime SDK mints it from the fulfillment return after the router has returned. There is no key to write under. On a bypass entry the poster does hold the id, but a bypass entry also holds the inbound id, so option A already covers it at no cost. The technique stays correct for the heavy analytics blob on the UPDATE, where it already runs and where the cap genuinely binds.

**D. Keep the sweep purely as a reconciliation backstop, and let the update fold promote a label the sweep wrote over evidence that did not exist yet.** Endorsed, and it is the second half of the decision rather than an implementation detail of the first. Tenet D6 states the shape directly: a low-frequency reconciliation sweep behind an event path is correct design, and it is a correctness backstop, never the primary mechanism. The sweep stays because events get dropped and because the in-batch pairer holds only the records it was handed. What changes is that a sweep result is no longer permanent: where the declaration arrives late, `paired_by` is promoted from `swept` to `declared`, on exact equality with the exchange's own user message, and never in the other direction (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1953`).

**E. Pay a channel read in the router so every turn knows the id before it posts.** Rejected. `findInboundMessage` is a `ListChannelMessages` and the router's own comments record that this read was deliberately moved out from in front of the first frame (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:4118`). Against a one second TTFF target, buying determinism for the metric by degrading the thing the metric measures is the wrong trade, and it degrades it for every turn to repair a label on some of them. A variant of E is already built and is why option A works at all: the processor pays the read AFTER the return and stamps the id on the UPDATE, which is what makes the promotion in option D have something to promote from.

**F. Remove the Amazon Lex entry from the turns that do not need it, so the channel flow dispatches with the inbound id in hand.** Out of scope here and named because it is the only change that makes this question disappear rather than shrink. ADR-036 records it as options B and C, and records that the measurement which would justify it has not been taken. If it is ever taken and the flow dispatches every untargeted turn, every turn declares its target on its CREATE and options A through E collapse into one deterministic path.

**A naming collision, said once so it does not trip a reader.** ADR-038 also has an option F and it is a different change: bounding the processor's late content match by the turn's own start instant. Every reference below and above that means that one names the record, as "ADR-038 option F"; a bare "option F" in this record is always the entry change above.

**G. Defer the sweep until the turn's own update has landed, instead of racing it.** Added by the first amendment of 2026-09-16 and endorsed there; stated here so the option list is a list of what was considered rather than a list of carriers. Options A through F are all one shape: they change WHAT carries the id, or WHERE the turn enters. None of them looks at the other party to the race, which is that `createExchangesFromDatabase` mints the moment a placeholder CREATE lands, whether or not the fact it needs exists yet.

It is a predicate rather than a mechanism. The sweep already LEFT JOINs the update row and already reads `am.agent_final_at` off the CREATE for `e2e_ms`, so "has this turn finished, or has it been long enough that it never will" is one more clause in a `WHERE` the query already has and needs no new join, no new column and no new pass.

**As built it is four disjuncts, and each one is a different way of saying the record is complete.** The declaration is already readable from whichever row carries it, so there is nothing left to wait for. Or the answer update has landed, so whatever it declared, including nothing, is the last evidence this pairing will get. Or the turn already finished on its CREATE, which is the `DIRECT` answer that has no update to come and would otherwise wait forever for one. Or the placeholder is older than any live turn can be.

**Cost, stated plainly, because it is real and it is not zero.** The exchange row is what carries `delivery_option` and `inbound_ms`, and it is what the ledger's `user_message` anchor reaches through (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/turn-events-backfill.ts:111`). Deferring the mint delays all of those by one window for every turn that takes one, which is a read-time latency an analyst sees as "the last few minutes are not populated yet". It also needs a bound: a turn whose update never arrives has to pair eventually, or the deferral converts a mislabeled pairing into a missing one, which is worse.

**The bound is derived from the deployment's own topology rather than picked.** It is the longest profile timeout, read live from the profile registry, times the invocations one turn may consume, plus an archival settle allowance. The invocation count is Lambda's default async retry of three attempts times the turn re-dispatch limit of two, both recorded in `turn-lock-release-on-failure.ts`; the settle allowance is two minutes covering the async retry backoff, the five second Kinesis batching window and consumer lag. On the default topology that is 90 seconds times six plus 120, which is 660 seconds, and it moves with a deployment that raises a profile timeout instead of quietly re-censoring that deployment's slowest turns. Erring long costs a late pairing; erring short costs a mislabeled one.

**Why it is better than any of A through F.** It is indifferent to the entry and to the deployment. An ordinary Amazon Lex turn, a battle round, a bypass entry and a default deployment all reach it at once, because the thing it fixes is on the reader's side of the boundary rather than on the turn's. Every carrier option reaches a subset of entries that varies with a feature flag, which is exactly the property the table above made visible.

**Built and deployed to dev on 2026-10-27; NOT VERIFIED on a deployment.** It is the deferral gate in `createExchangesFromDatabase`, verified by `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-sweep-waits-for-the-answer-it-is-pairing.test.ts`, which also pins the bound's derivation, that the sweep runs over every channel the batch touched rather than only ones with a CREATE, and that it runs BEFORE the update fold. Migration `053-a-sweep-waits-for-the-answer-it-is-pairing.sql` is `COMMENT ON` only and deliberately has no data step: no existing row changes meaning, and what moved is the CONTRACT of two columns, `exchanges.paired_by` and `turn_events.turn_id_source`. Nothing here is measured on a deployment.

## Decision

**Option A for the declaration, option D for the backstop, option G for the scheduling, and the turn's declaration is the single authority for what an answer answers. The resolution is "declare when known, defer rather than race, and reconcile what is left", and it is accepted as a compromise with a named exit, not as the end state.**

Option G was added on 2026-09-16 and the first amendment at the end of this record says why it belongs above the other two rather than beside them. All three are now built, unit-covered and deployed to dev on 2026-10-27, with no deployed test recorded; the second amendment records what the code turned out to say that this record had wrong. Points 1 through 7 below are as they were decided on 2026-09-15, corrected where they overstated their own scope; point 8 is added by the second amendment.

1. **The declaration is the authority.** Where a turn named the message it answers, that IS the pairing: no marker test, no time bound, no intervening-message test, because each of those exists only to guess at what the declaration states. This is already how both pairers behave and this record names it as the rule rather than as three coincidences.

2. **The declaration rides the earliest message event that can carry it.** Today that is the placeholder CREATE where the turn already holds the id, the closing UPDATE otherwise, and the `DIRECT` answer's own content where there is only one message. The carrier is chosen by what the delivery path has, never by which reader is being served, and archival folds all carriers to one column so no reader has to know which one a turn happened to have.

3. **The placeholder buys no read to declare, and exactly one path does.** A turn that does not already hold the id declares nothing on its placeholder and keeps exactly the content it posted before. The one component that does buy the id is the handler-answered `DIRECT` reply, which pays the memoized channel read (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3919`) because it posts once and has no later event to correct itself on; the owner priced that on 2026-09-11 and the comment above it records the price. Accuracy over latency is the standing rule on the pairing (`backend/lambda/src/lib/turn-declaration.ts:18`), and outside that one path it is not license to spend the person's first frame on a measurement.

4. **Inference remains, below the declaration, and says that it is inference.** `paired_by` records how each pair was made, and the headline TTFF partitions on it by name rather than on the absence of some other field (migration 042). A swept pairing keeps its exchange and loses only its vote in a latency nobody experienced.

5. **The sweep is a backstop, it waits for the evidence, and its verdict past the expiry is still not final.** It defers a pairing whose turn has not finished until the declaration is readable, the answer update has landed, the turn already finished on its CREATE, or the placeholder is older than any live turn can be. Past that bound it pairs on what it has, and where the declaration arrives afterward the label is promoted. The promotion only ever raises confidence, only on exact equality with the exchange's own user message, and a NULL on either side leaves the label alone. The precondition that used to stand here is narrower now rather than gone: on a default deployment an ordinary Amazon Lex turn has no declaration on either event, so nothing defers it usefully and nothing promotes it, and the sweep's verdict IS final for that population.

6. **The ledger does not get a second opinion.** `turn_events` continues to record `user_message` with a NULL `turn_id` and `v_turn_latency` continues to borrow the exchange's instant under `anchor_source = 'exchange'`. Migration 031's reasoning holds: a guessed key in a ledger whose purpose is auditability is worse than a NULL. What this decision changes is the confidence of the binding the ledger borrows, not where it borrows it from.

7. **The exit is named, so the compromise is not mistaken for a conclusion.** The ordinary Amazon Lex turn becomes deterministic in its CARRIER when the channel flow dispatches it, and not before. That is ADR-036's open question and this record is a second reason to take the measurement it waits on. The nearer exit, option G, is taken: the reader's answer no longer depends on when the reader looks, and nothing changed about where turns enter. What that leaves is a design whose labels are deterministic inside the staleness bound and whose COVERAGE still stops at the turns something knew the id for. Deterministic labeling over an incomplete population is what this is, and calling it anything else would be the same overclaim the second amendment corrects.

8. **The id a turn declares has to be the right one, and that is ADR-038's half.** A `declared` label is only worth its precedence if the id under it is the message the turn is answering. ADR-038 option F, built the same day, bounds the processor's late content search by the turn's own start instant so a repeated message queued behind a running turn cannot be taken for the one that started it. This record's part is to stop calling the result of a search the turn's own knowledge; that record's part is to make the search safe. Neither is complete without the other.

### Why the declaration is the best CARRIER and not merely the cheapest

This section was written as "why this is the best deterministic answer", which was the error the amendment names: it judged the best carrier and then described the result as the best answer, when the best answer was not a carrier at all. What follows is true of the carrier choice and is kept for that.

The cheap thing and the deterministic thing are the same thing here **for the turns it reaches**, which is unusual enough to state rather than assume. The fact is already known at placeholder time on every path that has it. The message is already being composed. The carrier already exists and already has a reader. So the change is to write a known fact one event earlier, and it removes a race rather than adding a mechanism to compensate for one. Every alternative that covers MORE turns covers them by paying for the id (option E), by deriving it (option B), or by changing how turns enter (option F), and the first two are worse on the axis this record cares about: option E degrades the measured quantity to measure it, and option B's failure mode is silent and identical to the defect.

**It is not the best answer for the turns it does not reach, and among the CARRIERS there is no best answer available for those today.** Option F is the only carrier change that would be, and it is blocked on a measurement and on a private-message route that does not exist. So on the ordinary Amazon Lex path this record endorses the cheapest carrier that is honest, and says so, rather than describing a partial fix as a deterministic design. The answer for those turns is option G, which is not a carrier.

## What proves it

- **Unit, built:** `backend/test/lib/a-placeholder-names-what-it-answers.test.ts` asserts the placeholder carries the declaration when the turn knows the id, carries none when it does not, keeps its correlation marker readable alongside, declares no phase (so the response is not closed the instant it opens), and folds to `messages.answers_message_id` through `transformToMessageRecord`.
- **Unit, built:** `backend/test/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival-backfill.test.ts` asserts the promotion fires only from `swept`, only on the exact id, and not at all where nothing was declared.
- **Unit, built, and the named verifying test for option G:** `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-sweep-waits-for-the-answer-it-is-pairing.test.ts` asserts the sweep does not mint while a bot message has no update and is inside the staleness bound, does mint once the update exists, does mint for a stale placeholder whose update never came, and mints immediately where the turn already declared. It asserts the modeled disjuncts and the SQL's disjuncts are the same set, so a disjunct added or removed in the source fails the test rather than being silently modeled. It proves it can fail by evaluating the pre-change SQL and watching the in-flight turn be minted. It pins the staleness bound to a derivation AND to a number, because a derivation that silently evaluated to five seconds would satisfy every structural assertion. And it pins the two things the deferral depends on operationally: that the sweep runs over every channel the batch touched rather than only ones with a CREATE, so a deferred turn gets another pass, and that it runs before the update fold.
- **Unit, built:** `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-backfilled-anchor-is-not-a-measured-one.test.ts` pins that a ledger anchor whose `turn_id_source` is `paired` is not counted as independently measured, so running the backfill over a window cannot readmit the swept population to the headline average.
- **Unit, built, on the sibling record's half:** `backend/test/lib/a-turn-answers-the-message-that-was-there-when-it-started.test.ts` pins ADR-038 option F, which is what keeps the id under a `declared` label from being a message the turn is not answering.
- **Unit, existing:** `backend/test/analytics-aurora/an-exchange-is-declared-not-inferred.test.ts` pins that both pairers obey a declaration and apply the same three heuristics where there is none. `backend/test/analytics-aurora/ttff-counts-only-measured-pairings.test.ts` pins the headline's partition on `paired_by`. `backend/test/lib/a-direct-answer-names-what-it-answered.test.ts` pins the content carrier. `backend/test/lib/the-close-out-carries-the-turn-not-its-compute.test.ts` pins the close-out allowlist. `backend/test/lib/turn-correlation.test.ts` and `backend/test/correlation-id-is-readable.test.ts` pin the marker's derivation and hold every SQL copy of its bounded grammar to the one TypeScript definition. `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-queued-message-that-was-answered-is-a-response.test.ts` pins the queued pass.
- **What is NOT proven by any of these.** The determinism property is guarded through the deferral gate's shape rather than end to end: every promotion case in `backend/test/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival-backfill.test.ts` reads after the fold, so none of them would fail if a reader's answer moved with the batch the update happened to arrive in, and the two-batch archival fixture that would assert it directly is described under "The determinism property" above and does not exist. Also unproven: that the share of turns the declaration reaches is large enough to move the headline, which is a read on a deployment; the deferral's read-time delay, which is estimated and not measured; and anything at all on a deployment, since none of the three changes is deployed.

## What is not yet true

- **The three changes are deployed to dev on 2026-10-27 and NOT VERIFIED there.** Every claim above about the fix rests on unit coverage, and no figure is measured on a deployment. The 45 percent reading is pre-fix, the recovery is unmeasured, and the deferral's read-time delay is estimated rather than observed.
- **The coverage of the fix is unmeasured.** It reaches only turns that already hold the inbound id at placeholder time. Nobody has counted what share of live turns that is, so nobody can yet say whether the 45 percent of turns with a ledger TTFF recovers because the declaration landed on the CREATE or because the promotion corrected the label afterwards. Those are different outcomes with different follow-ups, and the two are currently indistinguishable at read time: nothing reports the split between a pairing that was declared when it was minted and one promoted later. A counter for that split is the smallest thing that would make the fix's effect legible.
- **`paired_by = 'declared'` conflates THREE provenances of different strength, and nothing says which.** Naming them is the whole of the fix, so they are named:
  - **Declared-exact.** A bypass entry hands the id over as `AE.message.id` (`backend/lambda/src/router-agent-handler.ts:3520`). This is the turn's own knowledge and the label is accurate for it.
  - **Resolved-by-search.** An Amazon Lex turn that resolved the id got it from `findInboundMessage`, which takes the NEWEST exact content match from this sender within a bounded window (`backend/lambda/src/lib/inbound-message.ts:102`). It refuses rather than guessing when nothing matches and it matches the platform's own stored content rather than judging a phrase, so tenet D11 is satisfied. It is still a search.
  - **Judged-by-reading.** The queued pass writes `'declared'` (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1724`) from `answered_queued`, which the answer stamped from `foldedMessageIds` (`backend/lambda/src/lib/async-processor-core.ts:4549`) and which the triage READING produced. That is a model judgment recorded as data. ADR-033 is right that a recorded reading beats an inference from adjacency, and it is still not the same kind of fact as an id a caller handed over. `queued_as = 'folded'` distinguishes the population but says nothing about the provenance of the pairing inside it.

  Tenet D7's "say what a number counts" applies to a provenance label as much as to a count, and one label over three strengths is the same failure this record spends its length on.

- **`findInboundMessage` can still be wrong inside one turn's start window, and nothing downstream can correct it.** The search returns the newest exact content match. That holds when a turn answers the most recent thing the person said, and under the turn queue it systematically did not: the running turn is often answering an older message while newer ones wait, and "yes", "ok" and "do it" are exactly the messages people repeat. ADR-038 option F closes that population by bounding the processor's late call at the turn's own start instant, so a copy sent while the turn ran is refused and the turn declares the original or nothing. What is left is two identical messages sent BEFORE the turn began, which a content match cannot separate. Where that happens the row is `declared`, the headline admits it to the measured average, and the promotion is confirm-only, so no later evidence can move it. A wrong pairing minted this way is indistinguishable at read time from a right one, which is the condition `turn-declaration.ts` names as worse than a slow one. The exposure is the second provenance above and it is another reason the three need separate labels; this record's part is to stop calling the result of a search the turn's own declaration.
- **The population that can never declare is not separately reported.** With the deferral in place a row labeled `swept` means one of two things: the turn ran past the staleness bound with nothing having declared yet, or no component ever knew the id. The second is the honest residual and it should be countable. `ttff_reconstructed_count` (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/analytics-query.ts:2998`) counts both together. The deferral narrows the first of the two rather than removing it, so the split is still the smallest thing that would make the residual legible.
- **A declaration that contradicts a pairing is discarded rather than counted.** The promotion confirms a pairing and cannot correct one, which is right, and nothing records the contradiction either. A `swept` exchange whose answer declares a DIFFERENT user message is the one direct piece of evidence that the sweep guessed wrong, and today it is silent. Counting it needs no new fact and no new write.
- **The PROCESSOR-side declaration is contingent on a feature flag that is off by default, and the CREATE-side one is not.** With `-c turnQueue=true` unset, neither `holderRecordFields` nor `recordWhatThisTurnIsAnswering` runs, so no processor-side carrier is written on any entry. The placeholder declaration is unaffected by the flag, which is the correction the second amendment makes. What the flag decides is whether the UPDATE also declares, which matters to exactly one population: an ordinary Amazon Lex turn, the residual named above. Nobody has written down that a deployment's pairing quality on that population depends on the turn queue, and it is not a relationship a reader would expect. The table under "What is true today" is that relationship written down; it is not yet in `LATENCY-TARGETS.md`, where an operator would look for it.
- **An edit between the send and the lookup makes a turn silently declare nothing.** `findInboundMessage` compares the transcript against the message's stored content exactly (`backend/lambda/src/lib/inbound-message.ts:102`), so a person who edits their message after sending it, in the window before the lookup runs, produces no match. The function logs and returns an empty id (`backend/lambda/src/lib/inbound-message.ts:131`), the turn declares nothing, and the pairing falls back to the sweep. That is the safe direction and it is still invisible in the metric: the turn reads as one nothing ever declared, not as one whose message moved. Redaction and deletion are benign by contrast, and for a structural reason rather than by luck: archival writes them as SIBLING rows with their own suffixed ids (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:514-521`), so the CREATE row the pairing was minted against is untouched and the exchange keeps pointing at it.
- **A content declaration embeds a foreign `MessageId` inside another message's archived content, which is a new class of cross-message reference in an append-only store.** Every other thing in a message's content is about that message. `<!--turn:answers=...-->` is a pointer to a different row, written into text, and it is an IDENTIFIER rather than content: it is masked at the guardrail (`backend/lib/constructs/bedrock-guardrails.ts:68`), stripped from everything a person or a judge reads, and folded to a column at archival (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:846`). Saying so matters because the store is append-only and the referent can be redacted or deleted while the reference stays. That leaves a dangling id in archived text rather than a broken join, since the join runs on the folded column, but nothing today asks what a declaration naming a redacted message should mean.
- **The bounded marker grammar has one definition and SQL copies held in step by a test rather than by one source.** That is the pairing this repository uses elsewhere and it works, but it is not the same as one source.

## Consequences

- **ADR-022's finding reaches the analytics layer.** "The Amazon Lex entry has no message id" is why the pairing is declared where it can be and inferred where it cannot, and this record is where that consequence is written down rather than rediscovered.
- **ADR-036's open measurement gains a second reason to be taken.** If the Amazon Lex entry narrows, the declaration is available on every turn's CREATE and the inference paths become dead code rather than a documented fallback.
- **Migration 031's anchor decision stands and improves without changing.** `anchor_source = 'exchange'` keeps meaning "borrowed from the pairing", and the pairing it borrows becomes a statement rather than a reconstruction on the turns the fix reaches.
- **ADR-033's refusal of an inferred queued mapping stands.** The queued pass mints from a list the reading produced and the fold recorded, which is a declaration, and it is labeled `queued_as = 'folded'` so its wait is never averaged with a one to one turn (migration 052).
- **A race is a scheduling question before it is a carrier question, and this record is the worked example.** Six options were compared on where to write a fact, over a defect whose own description says the reader asks before the fact exists. Anything else in this tree that reads "X is not available yet when Y runs" is worth asking the same way round.
- **`LATENCY-TARGETS.md` owes its reader two relationships, not one.** The first is the turn-queue relationship: on an ordinary Amazon Lex turn a deployment's pairing quality depends on a flag that is off by default. The second is the deferral: the most recent minutes of the Latency tab are now under-populated rather than mis-populated, which reads to an operator as missing data and is the intended behavior. The document that explains which pairings vote says neither.
- **ADR-038 is the other half and the two must be read together.** This record decides when a declaration is trusted; ADR-038 decides that the id under it is the message the turn was answering when it started. A reader who has only this one will over-trust a `declared` label minted from a content search.
- **Revisit when** the deployment reports the declared-at-CREATE share, when Amazon Chime SDK forwards a flow-set attribute into Amazon Lex request attributes (ADR-022 names this as the condition that would let the router identify its own message), when ADR-036's entry measurement is taken, or when the deferral deploys and its read-time delay can be measured rather than estimated.

#### Amendment 1 (owner, 2026-09-16): the sweep waits for the update rather than racing it, and the placeholder declaration is the complementary half

"The message id is not known at placeholder time but it IS known by update time, so the sweep should wait for the update rather than race it."

**Everything above stays as written, because the reasoning is sound and the conclusion is too narrow.** The record diagnosed a CARRIER problem: which message event can carry the id, how early, at what cost. Options A through F are six answers to that one question, and the section "Why this is the best deterministic answer" compared them against each other and declared a winner. The comparison is right and the frame is wrong.

**The frame is wrong because a race has two parties and only one of them was examined.** The defect this record opens with is that a turn gets `declared` or `swept` depending on whether the UPDATE reached the archive before the sweep ran. That is stated correctly, in those words, and then every option moves the declaration earlier. Nobody asked whether the sweep has to run when it does. It runs the moment a placeholder CREATE lands, which is by construction the moment before the fact it needs exists, and the record's own mechanism paragraph says exactly that: "the cross-batch sweep therefore mints the pairing from the placeholder alone, at a moment when every carrier of the declaration is still NULL". The sentence describes a scheduling error and was read as a carrier shortage.

**It is the cheaper half as well as the more general one.** `createExchangesFromDatabase` already LEFT JOINs the update row (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1492`) and already reads `am.agent_final_at` off the CREATE (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/kinesis-archival.ts:1484`). The deferral is a predicate in a `WHERE` clause the query already has: do not mint from a placeholder whose turn has not finished, unless it is old enough that it never will. No new join, no new column, no new pass, and no message carries anything it was not already carrying.

**Why it is better than racing carriers, stated against the matrix.** Every carrier option reaches a population that varies with the entry and with the turn queue flag: bypass entries but not ordinary Amazon Lex turns, or turns with the queue on but not a default deployment. The deferral is indifferent to both, because what it changes is on the reader's side of the boundary. An ordinary Amazon Lex turn, a battle round, `@all`, the stop path and a default deployment all stop being raced at once. It is also the only one of the options that addresses the determinism property directly: the property's failing clause is "no reader's result depends on when the reader looks", and waiting for the fact is what makes the reader's result stop moving.

**What it costs, and it is a real cost rather than a formality.** The exchange row is not only a pairing. It carries `delivery_option` and `inbound_ms`, and the ledger's `user_message` anchor reaches the turn through it (`backend/lambda/src/analytics-aurora/turn-events-backfill.ts:111`), which is how `anchor_source = 'exchange'` is populated at all. Deferring the mint delays every one of those by one window on any turn that takes one, so an analyst reading the most recent minutes sees a tail that is not populated yet where today it is populated wrongly. The bound matters for the same reason: a turn whose update never arrives has to pair in the end, or the deferral has converted a mislabeled pairing into a missing one and traded a known error for a silence.

**The placeholder declaration still ships, and the amendment makes it more load-bearing rather than redundant.** It is the cheap complementary half. The deferral fixes the label a reader sees; the declaration is what supplies the fact the deferral waits for, and on a default deployment it is the ONLY declaration that exists outside the handler-answered `DIRECT` reply, because every processor-side carrier is behind the turn queue flag. The two halves answer different halves of the same race and neither subsumes the other.

**Status at the time of writing, superseded by amendment 2.** The deferral was in flight and untested when this amendment was written. It is now built, unit-covered and deployed to dev on 2026-10-27, and NOT VERIFIED on a deployment.

**What is rejected by this amendment, and why.** Not the declaration on the placeholder CREATE, which stays for the reason above. Not the promotion, which stays as the backstop for a declaration that arrives after even a deferred mint. What is rejected is the record's own claim that "there is no best answer available for those turns today": there was one, it was not a carrier, and it was available the whole time in a query the pipeline already runs.

#### Amendment 2 (2026-09-16): the record is reconciled to the code, and its matrix was wrong about which entries declare

**The three changes are built.** The placeholder declaration, the promotion and the deferral are all written with unit coverage naming them, and all three are deployed to dev on 2026-10-27. The record says Built where they are built and says NOT VERIFIED everywhere a figure would need a measurement on a deployment. Amendment 1's status line is superseded and kept above as the history of when the deferral was still in flight.

**The matrix under "What is true today" was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction that made the fix look smaller than it is.** It said that on a default deployment `@all`, a battle side, the alt, a handover, the stop evaluation and the task-answer repair all declare nothing, because the id stops at the router. The first half of that is correct: the id does stop at the router, in the sense that it never reaches the processor without the turn queue. The conclusion drawn from it is not, because the router is the component that composes the placeholder. `placeholderContent` reads `knownInboundMessageId` with no turn-queue gate, so every one of those entries declares on its own placeholder on a default deployment, and a duel side declares through `battlePlaceholderContent` for the same reason. The record asserted the opposite in a matrix while option A three sections later stated the ungated read correctly, which is how the error survived a reading.

**It may have been wrong when written rather than having gone stale.** The declaration on the placeholder and the matrix were composed together, and nothing in the file's history suggests a gate was removed afterward. That is worth recording because the lesson is not "the code moved": it is that a table of entry kinds is a snapshot of a mechanism, and a snapshot can be wrong on the day it is taken. The table is rewritten around the mechanism for that reason, as three questions asked in order, with the entry kinds derived from the answers rather than enumerated beside them.

**The genuine residual is smaller and sharper than the matrix implied.** It is an ordinary Amazon Lex turn that is not answered `DIRECT` from the handler, opens no task and does not queue. That turn is handed no id, is made to pay for none, and declares nothing on either event on a default deployment. It pairs `in_batch` where both halves land in one archival batch and `swept` otherwise. Everything else in the old matrix declares on its CREATE today.

**The deferral does not reach that residual and this record should not imply it does.** Waiting for evidence that never arrives produces the same reading later, and the staleness bound then expires and pairs it `swept` exactly as before. What the deferral fixes is a label decided by batch timing, for every turn that has evidence coming. What it costs is that the most recent minutes are under-populated rather than mis-populated, and the exchange row carries `delivery_option`, `inbound_ms` and the ledger's anchor, so all three are late together.

**The promotion was reassessed and kept.** With the sweep deferring, the obvious reading is that a late promotion is dead code. It is not: the deferral must expire, or an abandoned or failed turn leaves analytics entirely, and a turn that pairs at the expiry can still have its declaration arrive afterward. Removing the promotion would turn the staleness bound into a censoring boundary of its own, which is the same defect this record opens with, one size smaller.

**One more thing is built that the record did not name.** The backfilled population no longer readmits itself to the headline average: the measured predicate asks `turn_id_source IS DISTINCT FROM 'paired'` alongside the ledger anchor, so a ledger row bound through the exchange falls through to the exchange's own `paired_by` rather than counting as an independently measured anchor. It is verified by `backend/test/analytics-aurora/a-backfilled-anchor-is-not-a-measured-one.test.ts`, and migration 053 records the column contract for it.

**What this amendment does NOT change.** The inventory, the precedence rule, the rejection of options B, C, E and F, the three provenances under "What is not yet true", and the exit named in ADR-036. It changes what this record says is true about the code and what it says is true about itself.
