# ADR-061: A welcome is a workflow an event starts

## Status

**Accepted (2026-10-28). Built and deployed to dev**, except what "What is not built" lists, and the `welcome-workflows` e2e phase passed on dev on 2026-10-29 ("What proves it").

Built: an assistant's welcome lives on its version as its `assistant-joined` workflow, whose opening step carries opening text that may name the conversation's own fields, `{{role}}`, `{{examples}}` and the version's variables (ADR-060), and the room's conversation type selects the workflow (`welcomeWorkflowOf`, `renderWelcomeTemplate`). A line naming a field the room lacks is left out (decision 9). The welcome path reads whether the conversation's subject is onboarded, only to decide whether to start the intake (`onboarding-intake.ts`). The `memberWelcome` notice sends one person a targeted message and its email (`typed-conversation-notice.ts`, `conversation-management.ts`). The join recap is a model turn of the room's primary assistant, asked for through the router of the conversation's classification and targeted at the person just added (`join-recap.ts`).

Built and reused: a workflow step may declare an `action` the platform runs on entry with no model call. The run is claimed exactly once per entry (`claimStateAction`), the runtime takes the step's declared `onSuccess` or `onFailure` edge on the outcome, and the move is logged as runtime-driven (`state-actions.ts`, SPEC-TASK-STATE-TRANSITIONS FR29).

Built (2026-10-28) and deployed to dev: slice 1, the `prompt` welcome, the `workflow` welcome for `assistant-joined` and `conversation-opened` (since 2026-10-29 one welcome workflow, decision 9), conditioned edges and a step's `when` (ADR-060), `person-joined` from every path that adds a person, `invitation-sent` from the invitations function, participant roles from an opening event, a group, an invitation and a confirmed proposal, addressed and mentioned by sends and agent steps, `invitation-unredeemed` on a one-shot schedule the router creates, proposals with their confirmation card and apply endpoint, the join recap as `person-joined`'s default step, the intake as a step, an `agent` step that opens a task, the `host` participant-role source, one room per opening event, the incident watch (`alarm-state-changed`), and the visual workflow builder with each version's stored step layout and Preview run ("What is built"). Not built: the `resolveVia` participant-role source, an `agent` step's fan-out, a room message emailed to named people, and the meeting events and their deadlines (deferred until the meetings code lands on main).

**Amended (2026-10-27).** Six changes, each written into the decision it changes:

- **One event model.** The events here are the platform's single "something happened, then work" mechanism. The set covers a typed conversation an event opened, people joining, meetings and the effort to get people to join, each event with exactly one declared source that the router verifies on arrival, and an event that marks a deadline comes from a one-shot schedule the router creates when it runs the event that starts the thing it times (decision 1). The meetings designs use these events rather than a path of their own from EventBridge to the fan-out coordinator.
- **Reasoning agents assist these flows.** An event workflow may contain an `agent` step, a model-driven step under rules that keep the opening instant, the work bounded, metered and on the ledger, the tools read-only, and anything consequential behind a person's confirmation (decision 8).
- **Participant roles as audiences.** A send may address the holders of a participant role the conversation type declares, `{ participantRole: '<name>' }` (decision 3, and DESIGN-CONVERSATION-TYPES section 6.1).
- **Mentions in room messages.** A message the room reads may mention members by display name; every other fact about a person stays in targeted messages (decision 6).
- **Three welcome modes.** A version, and each of its conversation-type variants, chooses `welcome.mode`: `structured`, `prompt` or `workflow`. The workflow welcome of this decision is the third mode of that one setting (decision 9).
- **What it adds to IAM, and the order it is built in** ("IAM this adds", "Build order").

**Amended (2026-10-29): the welcome is one special workflow `WelcomeIntent` starts.** The three welcome modes of the 2026-10-27 amendment are one workflow: the version's `assistant-joined` workflow, selected by the room's conversation type, whose opening is a template step, a prompt step after it, or steps that greet and then continue (a targeted greeting, an intake question, an `agent` step). The version's separate `welcome` field and its mode are retired, and a room an event opens runs the `conversation-opened` workflow its type selects. Written into decision 9, which keeps its number.

## Context

**Why the welcome names nobody today.** It fires when the assistant joins (Amazon Lex `WelcomeIntent`), carries no sender, and is posted to the whole room. SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR10 keeps a name out of it because at that moment the creator's membership and the channel's Metadata have not converged, so a name resolved there races and is often wrong. The participant shape, written before the channel exists (`participant-shape.ts`), removes that race for the people the conversation was opened for, which is what makes decision 4 possible.

**The welcome is one case of a pattern the platform is growing.** In DESIGN-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS, the primary assistant posts when a meeting starts and when someone joins, and the notification bridge emails the people concerned. A typed conversation opened by an event welcomes each admitted person privately and emails them. A person added to a conversation is caught up by the join recap. A workflow step that assigns work tells the assignee. Each is the same thing: **something happens, then an ordered set of messages goes to chosen people, in the room and by email.** That is a workflow whose steps are the platform's, and the runtime for those steps exists.

**Some of these flows need reasoning, not only templates.** An incident room that opens should show what the deployment's health says before the lead asks, an arriving attendee's brief depends on their role and on state no template has seen, and a nudge to someone who has not joined reads better drafted than filled. A trigger per feature, such as a second target on the meeting lifecycle rule invoking the fan-out coordinator directly, would answer this for one feature and give the platform two event models for one pattern, which tenet D9 refuses. So there is one: the event starts the workflow, and a model-driven step inside it does the reasoning (decision 8).

**What a welcome can know, and at what cost:**

| Fact | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Conversation type | the channel-context row (ADR-053) | none: already read |
| Classification | the conversation's classification tag | none: already read |
| Host facts (`userName`, `participantProfile`) | the channel-context row written at creation | none: already read |
| Who the conversation was opened for | the participant shape: its `subject` and its `humans` | none: already read |
| Display name, clearance | the directory and the clearance resolution | one cached read each |
| Onboarded | the user-profile store (`user-profile-client.ts`) | one read, already made for the intake gate |
| Preferred language | the person's preferences (`person:preferredLanguage`, ADR-062) | one cached read |

## Decision 1: an event starts a workflow, and a welcome is the workflow `assistant-joined` starts

A workflow on an assistant's version may declare `on: <event>` instead of being selected by a turn's intent. The event starts it in the conversation where the event happened.

- **Events** are a closed, platform-declared set, and the platform's only "something happened, then work" mechanism: a feature that needs work to follow an occurrence declares or reuses an event here rather than wiring a trigger of its own. A new event is a platform addition, as a new variable source is (ADR-060). Each has exactly one declared source, and the router verifies each event against the record that answers it before it starts anything:

| Event | Its one source | Verified on arrival against | What it names | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `conversation-opened` | the canonical creator's event path (`typed-conversation-from-event.ts`), once a typed conversation that a declared `openedBy` event opened exists and the people its type admits are added. The ops room is the shipped case. The creator opens one conversation per opening event: it claims the event by the declaration and the event's own id before it creates anything (`claimOpening`), so a redelivered event opens no second room and starts no second run | the context row records a type that declares `openedBy`, and the conversation was created at the router's classification | the type, the person the opening event named (`event:person`, resolved to an AppInstanceUser), the label (`event:label`) | immediate |
| `assistant-joined` (the welcome) | the Amazon Lex `WelcomeIntent`, which the Amazon Chime SDK invokes through the AppInstanceBot's Lex configuration whenever the assistant is added to a channel, however it was added, and which reaches the router with no sender. A `WelcomeIntent` that carries a transcript is a misclassified reply and is handled as a turn, as the router does today | the router's own assistant is a member of the channel | the assistant | immediate |
| `person-joined` | each path that adds a person: the canonical creator for each person it admits, the share API (`share-conversation`), and the add-member path (`membershipHandler`, `conversation-management.ts`). Each names the event to the router (`sayThroughRouter`) once the membership exists. The join recap these paths ask for today becomes this event's `agent` step (decision 5) | the person is a member, read live from the Amazon Chime SDK | the person | immediate |
| `invitation-sent` | the path that writes an invitation's `Tokens` record (the invitations function, SPEC-CREDENTIAL-EXCHANGE section 4c, and for a meeting the apply endpoint of `invite_attendee`, **NOT BUILT**), once the record is written | the `Tokens` record exists, names this conversation, and is unredeemed and unrevoked | the invitee, the inviter, and the participant role and reason the invitation records. The invitations API records the `reason`, and a `role` only when the conversation's type declares it `invitable` (else `ROLE_NOT_DECLARED`) | immediate |
| `invitation-unredeemed` | a one-shot schedule the router creates when it runs the `invitation-sent` workflow, only when the version declares a workflow on `invitation-unredeemed`, at the deadline that workflow declares and never after the invitation's expiry | the `Tokens` record is still unredeemed, unexpired and unrevoked | the invitee, the inviter | deadline |
| `meeting-starting` | a one-shot schedule the router creates when it first runs a workflow in a scheduled meeting's conversation (its `assistant-joined`, which the apply of `create_meeting` causes, **NOT BUILT**), reading the start from the meeting record, only when the version declares a workflow on `meeting-starting`, at the lead time that workflow declares. The path that moves or cancels the meeting (**NOT BUILT**) asks the router to update or delete it | the meeting record's start still matches the schedule's version, and the meeting is not cancelled | the meeting, its expected attendees (SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR20) | deadline |
| `meeting-started` | the Amazon Chime SDK `MeetingStarted` lifecycle event on EventBridge, matched to its conversation by `ExternalMeetingId` (DESIGN-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS C.1) | the conversation's `activeMeeting` record names this meeting | the meeting | immediate |
| `meeting-joined` | the Amazon Chime SDK `AttendeeJoined` lifecycle event, the person resolved from the attendee's `ExternalUserId`; each join is its own event, and a rejoin carries `event:rejoin` | the meeting roster holds the attendee, and the resolved AppInstanceUser is a member of the conversation | the person who joined, `event:rejoin` | immediate |
| `alarm-state-changed` (the incident watch) | the CloudWatch alarm state change on the account's default bus, matched by one rule on the stack of each classification whose conversation types declare an opener that names the alarm its room watches (`watchesAlarm`), and only for this deployment's alarms by name prefix | the alarm's watch record and the watching room's own context row both name the room, at the router's classification and not archived, and the alarm's state read back from CloudWatch is still the state the change reported | the alarm (`event:alarmName`) and the state it entered (`event:alarmState`) | immediate |
| `expected-attendee-absent` | a one-shot schedule the router creates when it runs the `meeting-started` workflow, only when the version declares a workflow on `expected-attendee-absent`, at the deadline that workflow declares after the start, once per meeting | the roster at fire time; the event names only expected attendees who have not joined, and none if all have | `event:absent`, the expected attendees the roster shows have not joined, a person-list | deadline |

- **The router verifies every event, and starts nothing when the record does not hold.** Any principal that may invoke a router could otherwise name any event; the check against the record that answers it (membership, the roster, the `Tokens` record, the meeting record) is what makes an event's one source the only way to start its run. A refused event is logged with its kind and the conversation, never with a person's values.
- **A deadline is a one-shot schedule, never a poll (tenet D6).** The absence of an event cannot be event-sourced, which is the time-based case D6 allows, and the schedule is created once, when the thing it times starts: one per invitation, per scheduled meeting and per started meeting, never a periodic sweep looking for candidates.
  - **The router creates it**, when it runs the triggering event's workflow (the table above), because the router holds the active version and so knows whether any workflow declares the deadline event. A version that declares no workflow on the deadline event gets no schedule.
  - **Each classification owns a schedule group and a scheduler role.** The scheduler role's only grant is invoking that classification's own router, and the router's `CreateSchedule` and `PassRole` are scoped to its own group and its own scheduler role ("IAM this adds"). A schedule carries ids only: the conversation, the event, the natural key and the schedule's version, never a name, an address or text.
  - **On fire, before any model call,** the router refuses a conversation whose classification tag is not its own, that is archived or no longer exists, or whose named person is no longer a member or admitted, and then reads the one record that answers whether the deadline still matters (the invitation's `Tokens` record, the meeting record, the roster). It starts the workflow only if it does, so a redeemed invitation or a full meeting produces nothing.
  - **Resolving, archiving, deleting and offboarding delete schedules.** Redeeming or revoking an invitation, cancelling a meeting, archiving or deleting the conversation, and offboarding a person each ask the router of the classification concerned to delete the schedules keyed to that invitation, meeting, conversation or person (tenet D15: the store says whose job it is to delete). A schedule that survives is harmless because of the check on fire (tenet D5).
  - **As built, for `invitation-unredeemed`** (`event-deadlines.ts`; `scheduleInvitationDeadline`, `handleDeadlineFired`, `handleDeadlineCancel` in the router): each member classification's stack owns the group `<prefix>-<classification>-event-deadlines` and its scheduler role. The router schedules the deadline when it handles `invitation-sent`, whether or not a workflow runs on `invitation-sent`, at the `deadline.afterMinutes` the `invitation-unredeemed` workflow declares, brought forward to five minutes before the invitation expires, and schedules nothing for an invitation about to lapse. The schedule is named from a hash of the conversation and the invitation, so every path that resolves the invitation deletes it by name with no list call, carries `{ event, channelArn, invitationId }` alone, and deletes itself once it has fired (`ActionAfterCompletion: DELETE`). A redelivered `invitation-sent` finds the schedule already there. **Differs from this decision:** the schedule carries no version, because an invitation's deadline is never moved; only a meeting's would be. On fire the router checks the classification tag, the `archived` tag, the invitation's record (`pendingInvitation`), the primary, the declared workflow and its types, and that the inviter and the invitee are still members, read live, before anything runs. Revoking and archiving (every invitation of the conversation at this classification, read from the sparse index) name the deletion to the router of the invitation's classification (`cancelDeadlinesThroughRouter`), through the router invoke the invitations function and conversation management already held. **Differs from this decision, by decision:** redeeming an invitation and offboarding a person name nothing to a router, because the credential exchange and offboarding are sensitive principals and a router invoke on them is a grant they do not otherwise need; their schedule fires, finds the invitation redeemed or gone, starts nothing, logs that at info rather than as a warning because it is the expected end, and deletes itself. No path deletes a conversation except its retention, which the check on fire answers. **Decided: a create that fails is retried, then raised, never swept for.** The router tries the create three times in the same call, waiting 200 ms and then 800 ms, and where every attempt fails it emits `DeadlineScheduleFailed` (namespace `AgentEchelon/EventDeadlines`, by classification), which the classification stack's `<stack>-event-deadline-unscheduled` alarm watches and the one notifier delivers. No sweep and no poll stands behind it. The meeting deadlines are **NOT BUILT**: they wait on the meetings code.
- **As built, for the incident watch** (`handleAlarmStateChanged` in the router; `recordAlarmWatch`, `alarmWatchOf` in `channel-context-client.ts`; the `IncidentWatchAlarmChanges` rule in `assistant-profile-stack.ts`): an ops event may name the CloudWatch alarm its incident is about (`detail.alarmName`, the opener field `watchesAlarm`; `send-ops-event.mjs --alarm`). The creator records it on the room's context row (`watchedAlarm`) and in the alarm's watch record (`watch#alarm#<name>` in the context table), which names the newest room opened for the alarm. The rule hands the router the alarm's name and the state it reported and nothing else; the router reads the watch and the row, the room's tags, and the alarm's state by name (`cloudwatch:DescribeAlarms`, this deployment's alarms alone), and runs the workflow the primary's served version declares on the event for the room's type. A run is keyed by the alarm and the time CloudWatch says it entered its state, so a redelivered change starts nothing twice and each state the alarm enters is a run of its own. The platform ships `incident-watch`, which a version copies: it tells the room the state, then the ops assistant reads the deployment's health again and says what changed. **Decided:** the alarm state change is the incident watch's event, as slice 5 proposed. The meeting ending and a re-check after an investigation round, its other candidates, are **NOT BUILT**: the first waits on the meetings code, and the second on the rounds, which wait on fan-out (decision 8, rule 10).
- **Every event reaches the router of its conversation's classification.** A source names the event to that router through `resolveRouterArn` (`router-arn.ts`), the seam `sayThroughRouter` already uses, so one router runs every event workflow and no source holds an assistant session.
- **Whose workflow runs.** Every event except `assistant-joined` runs the workflow declared on the active version of the conversation's primary assistant (`primaryAssistantOf`, `conversation-primary.ts`), which the router resolves as it does for the join recap; a payload cannot name another. `assistant-joined` runs the joining assistant's own workflow, because each assistant opens for itself. **Decided:** a conversation type names no other owner for an event. The room's primary runs every event's workflow but `assistant-joined`, and another assistant in the room speaks for itself through its own `assistant-joined` welcome; an owner the type named would be a second way to choose a speaker beside `primaryAssistantOf`. **Decided, one exception: a step taken by a role.** An `agent` step may name `as: { role }`, and then that one step's turn is taken by the room's assistant of this classification holding the role, as itself, on its own vended session with its own grants (tenet D1), while the run, its claims, its version and its continuation stay the primary's. The router finds that assistant among the classification's live assignment records and the room's members (`assistantForStep`, `stepTaker`) and dispatches the turn as it (`workflowOwner`, `ownerBotArn` on the dispatch); the processor reads the step from the owner's active version and takes it only when it names the processor's own role (`stepTakenBy`), then hands the run back to the owner. It exists because a step can need a grant only one role holds: the platform's `ops-room` first look and `incident-watch` re-read name `read_deployment_health`, and the boundary refuses CloudWatch to every primary's session, so both name `as: { role: 'ops' }`. Where no assistant holding the role is in the room the step is never dispatched, so no turn runs and no tool is called: it posts its `fallback.absent` message (`ops-assistant-absent`, which says plainly that no ops assistant is in the room to read the health) and takes its failure edge. A turn the processor refuses (`stepTakenBy`) runs nothing either: the processor claims the step `refused` and hands the run back, and the router says the step's `fallback.send` and takes its failure edge (`continueAfter`, `agentStepDelivered`), so a run never ends as a success with nothing said for its step. Only a join recap ends `skipped` on a success edge (`nothingToRecap`); a step that reads the room's messages as context, such as the incident watch's re-read, runs in a room no person has spoken in yet. Validation does not refuse a version for such a step on a deployment that declares no such role, because a role can be added to a running deployment; it warns (`stepRoleWarnings`), and a step's `tools` are checked against the version's own tools only where the version's assistant takes it. **As built:** the step is read from the owner's active version, so an experiment arm of the owner serves its own steps but a step taken by a role reads the owner's active version.
- **One workflow per event per version.** Variation lives in the workflow's edges, not in several workflows competing for one event.
- **An intent pack declares no event workflow (decided).** A workflow on an event is one assistant's greeting and follow-up, so it lives on that assistant's version (`eventWorkflows`), is hashed into its `configId` and travels with its export, while an intent pack is a classification's taxonomy of what turns ask for. A pack that declares `eventWorkflows` is refused (`validateIntentPackBody`, `intent-pack.ts`).
- **It is the same workflow format**, validated by the same `validateTaskStateMachine`, with two added rules for an event workflow: every step is a platform step (decision 2) or an `agent` step under decision 8's rules, and the graph is acyclic, so a run is bounded by its step count rather than by `MAX_RUNTIME_MOVES_PER_TURN`.
- **A workflow declares the conversation types it runs in.** A workflow lives on an assistant's version, and one version serves several types, so validation cannot know which type's participant roles an audience names unless the workflow says. `types: [ops, ops-team]` makes validation check every `{ participantRole: '<name>' }` audience, `participantRole:<name>` variable and mention against those types' declarations, and the workflow does not run in a conversation of another type. A workflow that declares no `types` runs in every type; its role references are checked at run time only, resolving to nobody and logging where the type does not declare the role, and synthesis records a warning naming each such reference.
- A per-conversation-type welcome is a welcome workflow whose `types` name the type, and an existing welcome is carried into its workflows by `npm run migrate-legacy-welcome`, word for word (decision 9).

```yaml
workflows:
  welcome:
    on: assistant-joined
    initial: opening
    states:
      opening:                         # step 1: broadcast
        send: { to: room, template: built-in-opening }
        onSuccess:
          - { to: greet-new,  when: { person:onboarded: false } }
          - { to: greet-back, when: { person:onboarded: true } }
          - { to: done }
      greet-new:                       # step 2: targeted
        send: { to: subject, template: first-welcome }
        onSuccess: intake
      greet-back:
        send: { to: subject, template: welcome-back }
        onSuccess: done
      intake:                          # step 3: the onboarding intake
        action: { tool: start_intake }
        onSuccess: done
      done: { disposition: success }
```

## Decision 2: sending a message is a step action

A `send` step is a step action, declared in its own field the way a delivering step declares its template. Entering it renders the named message template (ADR-062) for the audience (decision 3) and posts it as the assistant through the router's notice path (`sayNotice`), with no model call unless the template declares `fill: voice`. It has everything FR29 gives an action:

- **Exactly once per entry.** The send is claimed before it is posted, so a redelivered event or a retried container does not greet anyone twice. That replaces any separate "already greeted" bookkeeping.
- **The outcome takes a declared edge.** A send that posts takes `onSuccess`. A send whose audience resolves to nobody is a success with nothing posted, so a group conversation passes through a `subject` step without a special case. A send that fails takes `onFailure` or the workflow's terminal failure state, and the room is never left waiting on it.
- **Logged as runtime-driven**, with the template and the audience kind, never the rendered text or a person's values.
- **It declares a turn kind.** A `platform` send declares `notification`. A `voice` render declares `welcome` at an arrival event (`conversation-opened`, `assistant-joined`, `person-joined`, `meeting-joined`) and `event_step` at every other event (SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR19).

```yaml
send:
  to: room
  template: ops-room-opening
  mention: { lead: event:person }      # {{lead}} renders the person's display name, as a mention
```

**A `mention:` binding names whose display name a room message says.** It maps a template field to a person or a person-list from platform state: `event:person`, `subject`, `participantRole:<name>` (ADR-060). Each person renders as their display name under decision 6's rules. A person-list (`event:absent`, or a participant role declared `holders: 'many'`) renders as a list joined by the target language's list format (`Intl.ListFormat`), capped at five names and closed with a count of the rest from a plural form of the template (ADR-062 decision 4). An empty list renders empty and drops its line. A field bound by `mention:` is the only person field a room section may carry.

**Order is the workflow's.** The broadcast is one step and the targeted greeting is the next, so whatever runs first is the step the author put first. The platform adds no ordering rule of its own.

**The intake is a step too.** Today the intake starts beside the welcome on the same event. In workflow form it is a step (`start_intake`, a registered action over `onboarding-intake.ts`), and the author decides whether it follows the targeted greeting, replaces it, or is left out. **As built** (`action: { tool: start_intake, to }`, `offerIntakeStep` in the router): the step offers the intake to the one person its event names (the subject at `assistant-joined`, the person who joined at `person-joined`, the person the opening named at `conversation-opened`), with no model call. It reads the intake as the join reads it, asks nothing of a person already onboarded or with nothing left to ask, records the intake as theirs only where no other person's is in progress in the conversation (`startOnboardingIntake`), because only the recorded person's messages answer it, and targets the first question at them alone. In `workflow` mode the join starts no intake of its own, so the workflow's step is the intake; the platform's `opening-and-subject` offers it after greeting the subject.

**Edges choose by condition.** An outcome edge may be an ordered list of `{ to, when }` alternatives over ADR-060 variables. The runtime takes the first whose `when` holds, and the last alternative carries no `when`. This is the same `when` ADR-060 decision 6 puts on a model-driven edge, where it gates the move the model asked for; here the runtime is choosing. An unresolved variable makes every condition false (ADR-060), so a failed read takes the unconditional edge.

## Decision 3: a send names its audience

| Audience | Posted as | May name a person | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| `room` | one message the room reads | as a mention by display name only, and no other fact about a person (decision 6, ADR-060 decision 7) | - |
| `{ room: true, email: { people: <variable> } }` | one message the room reads, emailed to the people a variable names | as a mention only; in the email to a person who is not a member, every mention renders empty | the room reads it; the people named receive it by email |
| `subject` | a message targeted at one person | yes | the participant shape's `subject`: the one person a single-person conversation was opened for |
| `each-person` | one targeted message per person | yes | at `assistant-joined`, the `humans` in the participant shape; at a later event, the channel's live membership |
| `joined` | a message targeted at the person the event names | yes | the person who joined (`person-joined`, `meeting-joined`), once the router has verified the membership (decision 1) |
| `{ people: <variable> }` | one targeted message per person | yes | the people a declared variable resolves to: a meeting's expected attendees, a task's assignees, `event:person`, `event:absent` |
| `{ participantRole: '<name>' }` | one targeted message per holder | yes | the members who hold that participant role in this conversation, as the conversation type declares and resolves it (DESIGN-CONVERSATION-TYPES section 6.1): a room's `lead`, a meeting's `organizer`. A role the workflow's declared `types` do not declare, or declare with `confers.audience: false`, is refused at validation; at run time it resolves to nobody. Built for a `send` and an `agent` step |

**A participant role is not an assistant role.** A participant role is a function a person holds in one conversation, declared on the conversation type. An assistant role (the `{role}` welcome token, `ops-premium`'s role, ADR-048) names what an assistant is, and an IAM role is a principal. The audience, the variables and the context-row field all say "participant role" so the three never meet.

**A targeted message** is posted in the conversation as the assistant with the Amazon Chime SDK `Target` set to that person, so the messaging service delivers it to that person alone. That is the read boundary. `privateReaderOf` (`async-processor-core.ts`) is the second layer: it keeps the message out of other members' context, and the summary exclusion (`analytics-aurora/summary-updater.ts`) keeps it out of the conversation summary. It is emailed under ADR-062 decision 4's language rule.

**Each event declares its notify kind** for the notification bridge (`NOTICE_KINDS`, `notice-preferences.ts`): `conversation-shared` for `conversation-opened`, `assistant-joined` and `person-joined`; `invitation`, which is transactional, for `invitation-sent`; `nudge` for a nudge a person confirmed after `invitation-unredeemed` (built: the reminder the invitations function sends, `nudgeOperation`) or `expected-attendee-absent`; and, **NOT BUILT** because the meetings code has not landed on main, `meeting-reminder` for `meeting-starting` and `meeting` for the start and join messages of `meeting-started` and `meeting-joined`. The three new kinds are not transactional, so every send of them honors the person's preference for that kind and carries the bridge's one-click unsubscribe (SPEC-NOTIFICATION-BRIDGE FR19). A proposal addressed to the person who confirms it is a targeted message in the room, kind `conversation`. A targeted message is emailed only to a person who did not open the conversation themselves.

**A `{ people: <variable> }` audience, a `{ participantRole: '<name>' }` audience, a `joined` audience, and a room message's `email.people`, are bounded:**

- The variable is a platform-trust `person-list` (ADR-060). A member-trust variable, or one that is not a person-list, is refused as an audience at validation and resolves to nobody at run time.
- Each person must be a member of the conversation, or a person the event is admitting who has been admitted: admission against the conversation's classification and the type's issuer list has run and let them in (a meeting's expected attendee being let in). A person refused by either is skipped and is never emailed, and a person whose admission has not run yet is not yet anyone's audience.
- No source lists a group's members, so a workflow cannot reach people outside the conversation by naming a group. A participant role resolved from a group names only the group's members who are members of the conversation.
- A version names a variable or a participant role, never a person: the list is resolved from platform state at the event.
- A multi-person audience is capped (default 10, declared per step). An audience larger than the cap sends nothing and takes the step's `onFailure` edge, which an author points at a room step.
- **In a mixed-trust room, a holding is said only where guests may hear it.** On a type that admits an external or guest issuer (SPEC-CONVERSATION-SECURITY Layer 7, FR16), a room message may mention a participant role's holder only when the role declares `visibleToGuests: true` (DESIGN-CONVERSATION-TYPES section 6.1); otherwise the message is targeted to members of an internal issuer, or the line drops.

**Meetings** use the room audience emailed to people: a meeting's start and each join are posted to the room once and emailed to the expected attendees, within SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR11b (the assistant's initiative is bounded) and FR19 (each such post is a declared turn kind). `meeting-starting` reminds the expected attendees with a targeted message each (SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR31), and the arrival brief (FR4), the chase of an absence (FR33) and the chase of an unredeemed invitation (FR32) are `agent` steps (decision 8).

## Decision 4: whom to send to is looked up, never guessed

The subject and the people come from the participant shape recorded before the conversation was created, from the event itself, from a declared variable, or from a participant role the conversation type declares and resolves from records (DESIGN-CONVERSATION-TYPES section 6.1). They never come from the first member listed: naming `humans[0]` in a group is the mistake `participant-shape.ts` exists to prevent, and live membership has not converged when an assistant joins.

## Decision 5: where an event workflow runs, and what it records

- **The router runs `platform` steps.** `assistant-joined` (as `WelcomeIntent`) arrives at the router, which already posts the welcome, and every other event is named to the same router by its source (decision 1). It runs the event workflow's `platform`-filled sends in order with the step-action runner, so the opening stays instant (SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR2): no model call before the first message the room reads. The task runner is processor-only (`runStepActions` takes a `TaskLoopContext`, `state-actions.ts`), so the router runs an event workflow with its own runner over the event run's task record (`runEventWorkflow`, "What is built"), and a `send` posts on the router's speak session (`sayWorkflowSend`).
- **A `voice` step and an `agent` step are handed to the processor** as one turn of the assistant, dispatched the way the join recap is dispatched today (`dispatchJoinRecapTurn`, `join-recap.ts`): an asynchronous router turn that names the conversation, the person it is for and the event run, from which the router resolves the speaker and composes the request. The turn declares its kind (decision 2 and decision 8, rule 4). Once per-person fan-out exists, a per-person `agent` step goes to the fan-out coordinator's event entry instead (decision 8, rule 8).
- **The processor completes the step it was handed.** When the turn posts, fails or is refused, one conditional write on the event run's task record records the step's outcome and takes its edge, and the continuation (any following `platform` steps) is named back to the router through the `sayThroughRouter` seam, which runs them. The deadline is enforced in the processor: the turn is aborted at `deadlineMs`. A processor that dies before the write is caught by its on-failure destination, which posts the step's fallback and takes `onFailure`.
- **A step's outcome is claimed once, so a late reply never posts twice.** The fallback and the turn's own output each post only after winning a conditional write on the step's entry in the task record. Output that loses (a model reply arriving after the fallback, or a fallback racing a reply that already posted) is discarded and logged with the step and the event, never with its text.
- **Metering reuses the join recap's rule.** A `voice` render and an `agent` step are turns no person asked for, so they are metered as the join recap is: `machineWritten` on `evaluateAbuseGate` (`abuse-controls.ts`), which spends no person's hourly rate limit or per-user budget and is counted, and refused when spent, by the deployment's global budget (SPEC-ABUSE-CONTROLS FR3). One rule covers both. A refused meter takes the step's fallback. The volume of event runs is bounded by their sources rather than by a new meter: one run per event's natural key (below), one schedule per timed thing, at most one nudge per person per meeting or per invitation (decision 8, rule 6), the audience cap (decision 3), and SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR17's cap on how many arrivals of one meeting run at once.
- **The join recap becomes `person-joined`'s `agent` step.** Where a version declares no workflow on `person-joined`, the router runs the platform's default: one `agent` step addressed to `joined`, whose prompt is the shipped `join-recap` prompt template (ADR-062), which today's `joinRecapRequest` copy maps to until it is saved as the assistant's own template. A version that declares a `person-joined` workflow replaces the default, and the share and add-member paths stop dispatching a recap of their own.
  - **As built** (`join-recap`, `withJoinRecap`, `personJoinedWorkflowOf` in `event-workflow.ts`; `handlePersonJoined` in the router): the recap is an `agent` step that reads the room's recent messages (`reads: messages`), as the room's assistant reads them under its own grants with every targeted message left out (`privateReaderOf`), addressed to the person who joined. Where a version declares no `person-joined` workflow, or none for the room's type, the router runs the platform's `join-recap` for each person it verified. **Differs from this decision, by decision:** a version that declares a `person-joined` workflow does not replace the recap. Its run takes the recap after its own steps, before each terminal step, unless the workflow writes a recap of its own, so declaring a workflow never costs a joiner the recap, and the workflow's sends still come first. A version's workflow replaces the `memberWelcome` notice: no source sends its own welcome to a person a run owns. Every source that adds a person reaches the recap this way, the add-member path and the creator included; the share asks for the router turn of its own (`dispatchJoinRecapTurn`) only where the naming failed. A recap with nothing a person wrote before the joiner arrived, as in a room the creator just opened, calls no model, posts nothing and records `skipped`.
- **One path for a person's welcome.** A creation path names the event to the router (`sayThroughRouter`, `say-through-router.ts`) instead of sending its own `memberWelcome`. Where an assistant's version declares a workflow for that event, the separate `memberWelcome` call is retired, so a person receives one message and one email, not two.
  - **As built, for an invitation:** the invitation email stays the invitations function's own, whether or not a version declares an `invitation-sent` workflow, and a workflow's send at that event emails nobody (`workflowSendEmailKind`). The email is transactional: it carries the invitation's link, which is the credential, to the one address the inviter typed, and neither is stored anywhere. A workflow send's email goes through the notification bridge's directive, which admits only the kinds a person can mute (`DIRECTIVE_NOTICE_KINDS`) and finds the address in the directory, where an invitee has none. Admitting `invitation` there would make a transactional email mutable, and handing the address and the link to the router would put an address and a credential into an event's payload, which carries ids only. So an invitation sends one email, and an `invitation-sent` workflow's sends are read in the conversation.
- **One welcome per person per arrival, decided from configuration (as built).** A conversation an event opens has two paths that can greet the people it was opened for, the join (`WelcomeIntent`) and the creator's events, and they run concurrently, so which one owns a person's welcome is read from the version and the conversation's type, never from which finished first (`arrivalWelcomeOwner`, `event-workflow.ts`). The room's opening (a structured opening, a `prompt` welcome's turn, a workflow's room sends) is said once to the room, addressed to nobody and emailed to nobody; a person's welcome is the one message addressed to them, with its email.

| The version, in a type an event opens | The room's opening | The lead's welcome | Everyone else the creation admits |
|---|---|---|---|
| `workflow` on `conversation-opened` | that run's room sends; the join posts nothing and starts no intake | that run's send to `event:person`; the creator names the lead in no `person-joined` (the router returns the lead as the run's) | the `person-joined` run where one is declared, else `memberWelcome` |
| `structured` or `prompt`, with a `person-joined` workflow for the type | the join's opening, as always | the `person-joined` run | the `person-joined` run |
| `workflow` on `assistant-joined`, with a `person-joined` workflow for the type | that run, with no subject: its `subject` steps address nobody, as in a group | the `person-joined` run | the `person-joined` run |
| any mode, with no `person-joined` workflow for the type | the join's opening, and its intake or `subject` greeting where they apply | `memberWelcome` | `memberWelcome` |

  Where a `person-joined` run owns the people a room was opened for, the join starts no intake, because an intake addresses the subject and would be a second welcome for the same arrival; the room's opening is never replaced, and no question waits on anything. The `person-joined` workflow offers the intake as a `start_intake` step addressed to the person who joined, where its author puts one (decision 2).
- **An event run is recorded on a task record**, because the exactly-once claim, the transition log and the ledger all key on one. The record is marked as an event run: never returned as open work (the reads of ADR-024), never counted as work in task analytics, and expiring after the run ends, once its transitions are archived (decision 8, rule 11). Its transition log is how an operator sees which greeting a person received and why.
- **The claim is keyed `<event>#<natural key>#<assistant>`**, so a redelivered event or a schedule that fires twice starts no second run, and two assistants opening one conversation each run their own welcome:

| Event | Natural key |
|---|---|
| `conversation-opened` | the conversation |
| `assistant-joined` | the conversation |
| `person-joined` | the conversation and the person |
| `invitation-sent`, `invitation-unredeemed` | the invitation |
| `meeting-starting` | the meeting and the schedule's version, so a moved meeting's reminder is its own run |
| `meeting-started`, `expected-attendee-absent` | the meeting |
| `meeting-joined` | the meeting, the person and the join's sequence (the lifecycle event's own id), so a redelivery dedupes and a rejoin is its own run |

- **No greeting twice.** The first real turn's by-name greeting (`firstTurnGreetingDirective`, `async-processor-core.ts`) is suppressed for a person an event run already greeted by name.

## Decision 6: FR10 is narrowed, not dropped

**A message the room reads may mention members by display name, and every other fact about a person stays in targeted messages.**

- **A mention is a display name and nothing else.** A room message may insert `person:displayName` of a person resolved from the participant shape, from the event, or from a participant role the conversation type declares, bound by a `mention:` field (decision 2), and of no one else. Clearance, whether a person is onboarded, their language, their preferences, onboarding answers and host facts (`host:userName`, `host:participantProfile`, `host:role`) stay in targeted messages (ADR-060, decision 7).
- **Only a member is mentioned.** The resolver checks the person against the conversation's live membership from the Amazon Chime SDK (tenet D7) when the message is rendered, with one exception: at `assistant-joined`, a person in the participant shape's `humans` counts as a member, because live membership has not converged then and the shape was written before the channel existed (decision 4). Every other mention, at every other event, is checked against live membership. A person who is neither a member nor, at `assistant-joined`, in the shape's `humans` renders empty, and the line drops under ADR-060 decision 5's rule. A version never names a person, so a mention can only follow a resolution from platform state.
- **A display name inserts and never decides.** It stays member trust (free text from the identity provider): stripped and capped like any member-trust value, and no condition reads it (ADR-060, decision 2).
- **A mention is rendered inert.** The name is inserted with every address token, `@` handle, marker syntax and link neutralized, so a display name such as "@ops-assistant" or one carrying a marker cannot address an assistant, target a member or carry a link.
- **A mention by an assistant invokes no assistant.** The Amazon Chime SDK does not deliver a message an `AppInstanceBot` authored to another assistant's Lex ([ADR-023](./023-battle-round-coordination.md)), a mention renders as the neutralized name rather than as an addressing `@` handle, and no post-processing rule dispatches a turn on an event workflow's message. A mention names a person to the room; it does not address, target or summon anyone.
- **Why this is safe.** FR10 kept every name out of the welcome because the sender is unknown at `WelcomeIntent`, a race the participant shape removes (decision 4). A member's display name is already visible to every member of the conversation in its roster, so mentioning it discloses nothing the room did not have. The privacy rule is about facts, not names, and the facts keep their rule.

**What still holds.** The no-name rule binds every room message except as a mention above. A condition on a person-describing variable still selects only a targeted message. A targeted send may carry any variable about the person it is addressed to. The same change amends:

- SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR10 and its non-goal, and FR2, so that FR2 binds the first message the room reads and a model-written welcome (the `prompt` mode, a `voice` step or an `agent` step) may follow it;
- SPEC-BILINGUAL-CONVERSATIONS FR8, so that a greeting in a language with no written variant may be a `voice` render, after an instant `platform` opening;
- SPEC-TASK-STATE-TRANSITIONS, for event-started workflows, the `send` step, the `agent` step and conditioned outcome edges (its FR30);
- SPEC-CONVERSATION-TYPES, and the stale comments in `welcome-orientation.ts` that named `WelcomeConfig` and a `welcomeTemplateForRole` that does not exist (corrected as built, and `WelcomeConfig` retired from the conversation type).

## Decision 7: one editor, because it is one format

Event workflows and intent-selected workflows share a format, so the console edits both with the workflow editor. It is generalized from today's welcome guide (`WelcomeEditorGuide.tsx`: the fields with what each holds, insertion, and a preview) to every step: a `send` step's audience, template and mentions, an edge's conditions, a model-driven step's prompt, placeholder and requirements (ADR-031), and an `agent` step's prompt, tools, proposals, audience, deadline and fallback (decision 8). Each step supplies only its sample contexts and the variables its call site resolves.

- The preview renders through the same function the runtime uses, imported from the backend module as the welcome preview is today, so the line-drop rule has one implementation.
- Variables are inserted from a keyboard-operable list with an accessible name each.
- Each variable's trust, and whether it may decide, are shown as text, never by color alone.
- The preview is a live region.

Messages and email are built first: the welcome and the event workflows of the build order, with the `send` step. A `send` step inside an intent-selected workflow, telling an assignee, a reviewer or a requester, follows.

**As built, the visual workflow builder** (built and deployed to dev, "What is built"): the event workflows are edited on a canvas of their steps, from a palette, with the selected step's controls as its inspector. The inspector is the list editor's own controls and both views make the same edits, so there is one editor in two views; the list stays as the form view. Where each step sits is the version's layout, a body beside the persona that nothing the version does reads. Preview run walks a workflow through the runtime's own runner on the server, with sample values, saying nothing and writing nothing.

## Decision 8: an `agent` step lets a reasoning agent assist an event workflow

**Built** (slice 1, and proposals with slice 4; "What is built"). An event workflow may contain an `agent` step: a model-driven step whose turn reads, reasons and posts, under these rules. Its fields:

```ts
agent: {
  prompt: TemplateName                       // an ADR-062 template of kind `prompt`, selected in ADR-062 decision 3's order
        | { fromRole: 'brief'; else: TemplateName };  // the brief the person's participant role confers, else this prompt
  tools?: ToolName[];                        // read tools only: a subset of the assistant's profile tools
  to: Audience;                              // decision 3
  deadlineMs: number;                        // bounded by the platform maximum
  fallback: { send: SendStep };              // a `platform`-filled message template (ADR-062 decision 5)
  propose?: ProposalTool[];                  // state-changing tools it may propose, never execute
  workflow?: MachineKey;                     // a task the runtime opens before the turn, whose first step the turn runs
}
```

A prompt is a template of kind `prompt` in the document template store (ADR-062 decision 1): instructions to the assistant, with declared fields, selected assistant first, then the classification's, then the shipped one. It is never text on the workflow.

1. **The room's opening is instant and comes first.** Where a workflow posts to people who have just arrived (`conversation-opened`, `assistant-joined`, `person-joined`, `meeting-joined`), its initial step is a `platform` `send`, and validation refuses an `agent` step reachable before the first `send`. No model call stands between the event and the first message the room reads (SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR2). A workflow on an event nobody is waiting on (`invitation-sent` and the three deadline events) may start with an `agent` step. The default `person-joined` workflow (decision 5) is the one exception: it is the join recap, targeted to the new member, and posts nothing to the room.
2. **It has a deadline and a declared platform fallback.** The step declares `deadlineMs`, bounded by a platform maximum, and a `fallback`: a `send` of a `platform`-filled message template (ADR-062). Past the deadline, on a failed model call, on a guardrail block or on a refused meter, the fallback is posted and the run takes `onFailure`. Validation refuses an `agent` step with no deadline or no fallback, so a person is never left with nothing.
3. **It is metered as the join recap is.** `machineWritten` against the deployment's global budget (decision 5), one charge per run of the step; a fan-out counts one charge per leaf. A refused charge takes the fallback.
4. **It is a named, timed turn on the ledger.** The turn declares the kind `event_step` (built, beside `welcome` and `notification`) and carries the workflow, the step and the event, so archival pairs it with nothing and the latency grid groups it under its own label rather than under `undeclared_turn` (SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR19). It records its outcome on the ledger: `posted`, or `fallback:deadline`, `fallback:model_error`, `fallback:guardrail` or `fallback:meter`. The output is one response, which may be split across continuation messages: a long output is posted in ordered parts by the one splitter every long message uses (`sendInParts`, `lib/chime/send.ts`), and the step records one outcome for it, `posted` only when every part posted and `post_failed` when any part was refused, which says the fallback and takes `onFailure`. A fan-out adds one leaf row per person (DESIGN-MULTI-AGENT-STRANDS-RUNTIME, section 8). No metric is emitted beside the ledger (tenet D7).
5. **It reads as the assistant, within the assistant's grants, and executes only read tools.** The turn runs on the assistant's own vended session (tenets D1 and D4), with the tools the step names, which must be a subset of the assistant's profile tools and are filtered the way a workflow state's `tools` are (SPEC-TASK-STATE-TRANSITIONS FR28). Validation refuses a tool whose registry entry declares `changesState: true` (`tool-registry.ts`) unless the step lists it in `propose`, and a tool listed in `propose` is intercepted and never executed: an `agent` step executes read tools only. **Differs, as built:** `propose` names one op from the platform's registry (`PROPOSAL_OPS`), not a tool the model calls; the turn's whole output is the proposal's draft (a reminder's note, a new conversation's label), and the runtime records it and offers it, so nothing the model writes is executed or chooses the act, and a model with no tool use proposes the same way. The op's fixed arguments (the invitation, the conversation type in `opens`) come from the event and the step, never from the draft. Opening the declared `workflow:` task is the runtime's act before the turn, never a tool call the model makes. It adds no grant and no principal: a step that names `read_deployment_health` reaches telemetry only because the ops assistant's own grant already selects it.
6. **Its input depends on who reads its output.** Where the output or the proposal reaches only members reading the room (a room post, a message targeted to a member, a proposal to a member), the input is the step's prompt, the event's fields and the variables it names, passed as delimited data (ADR-062, decision 5), and the conversation as a turn reads it, with targeted messages kept out by `privateReaderOf`. Where the output or a proposal it drafts reaches a person not reading the room (a nudge to someone who has not joined, an email to an address with no membership), the step receives no conversation history: only its declared variables, as delimited data, as ADR-062's `voice` render does. **As built** for `send_nudge` (`agentStepIsNarrowed`): the persona alone is the system prompt, the invitation's names, title and reason are the prompt's data, the step runs no tools (validation refuses them), and a draft carrying a link, an `@` handle or an email address posts the fallback (`fallback:bounds`). Its output passes the output guardrail, `stripMessageMarkers` and the per-type caps, and carries no link but the platform's own (the conversation's link and the bridge's unsubscribe), so room content and an inviter's words cannot steer what an outsider receives.
7. **Initiative and consequence are defined once, here.** A targeted message to a member, and the notification bridge's email of it, is initiative: an assistant may send it on its own (SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR11b). An email to an address with no membership, an invitation, and creating anything (a meeting, a conversation) is consequence, and is a proposal a person confirms (FR11). On its own initiative the step may only post into the room or target a member.
8. **A proposal names who may apply it.** The step addresses each proposal to the person who confirms it, by participant role or variable (the PROPOSE-AND-CONFIRM pattern, DESIGN-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS B.2). The proposal records its addressee's AppInstanceUser id. The apply endpoint authorizes `callerPersonId` against that id and against the participant-role holdings on the context row at apply time, refuses a session whose bearer is an assistant, is single-use, and expires. At most one nudge is proposed per person per meeting or per invitation. **As built** (`proposals.ts`, `proposals-api.ts`, the processor's `eventStep` branch, the chat client's `ProposalCard.tsx`): a proposal is a single-use `proposal` record in the token store, written on the assistant's session (the turn acts as the assistant, so the processor's own role holds nothing on the store) and naming its addressee's AppInstanceUser; the targeted message that offers it carries its handle on Metadata, which only the addressee receives, and the chat client draws a confirmation card from it. `POST /proposals/apply` refuses a guest, a link session and an assistant's session, refuses anyone but the addressee (in the handler and again in the consuming update's condition), re-checks the caller's membership as the caller and a role addressee's holding on the context row, claims the record once (`applying`) before its act runs, records `applied` only once the act was done, releases the claim when the act is refused outright so its addressee may apply it again, and records `failed` for an act that did not answer unless asking again cannot do it twice (a reminder, which is released) (`settleProposal`, `releaseProposal`; verified by `backend/test/lib/a-proposal-is-applied-once-by-its-addressee.test.ts`), and refuses it past its expiry (three days, never past the invitation's). One reminder per invitation is held by the invitations function's transaction (`nudgedAt` on the invitation's record), and the deadline that drafts it fires once per invitation. A proposal may be addressed to a one-holder participant role every declared type gives one holder. Two ops are built: `send_nudge` at `invitation-unredeemed` (the inviter types the invitee's address on the card, used for that one email and stored nowhere; the reminder carries a second link beside the first, an alias record naming the invitation's one record, so either link redeems until that record is spent, and spending or revoking it ends both) and `open_conversation` (the classification's typed creator opens the step's declared type for the person who confirmed). `create_meeting` and `invite_attendee` are **NOT BUILT**: they wait on the meetings code.
9. **Its output follows the audience rules.** The step declares `to:` with the audiences of decision 3. Every part of a response split across continuation messages goes to the same audience, so a step addressed to a participant role reaches that role's holders in every part. A room audience carries no fact about a person beyond a mention (decision 6). On a type that admits an external or guest issuer, validation refuses an `agent` step with tools whose `to:` is `room` (SPEC-CONVERSATION-SECURITY Layer 7, FR16), because what a tool read is not known to be guest-visible.
10. **It runs in one of two places.** As one turn of the assistant in the processor, handed over as a `voice` step is (decision 5). Or, when the work is a fan-out of read-only leaves (one per person, or one per specialty of an investigation round), at the fan-out coordinator's event entry (DESIGN-MULTI-AGENT-STRANDS-RUNTIME, section 3.1), the collate node posting as the assistant. **Fan-out depends on sub-agent identity, which SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS ("What Blocks This, In Order", row 1) lists as BLOCKED** (DESIGN-MULTI-AGENT-ORCHESTRATION, decision 2). Until it exists, an `agent` step runs as the assistant, one turn per run, and per-person work is one run per person because the event is per person (`meeting-joined`) or one turn addressing each person in sequence. The incident watch's rounds are not an `agent` step until their events are in decision 1's set (DESIGN-MEETING-ONBOARDING-AND-INCIDENT-SUBAGENTS, section 4.2).
11. **The graph stays acyclic and bounded, and the run is archived.** An `agent` step is one node. The runtime takes its outcome edges (`onSuccess` when the turn posted, `onFailure` after the fallback), with ADR-060 `when` conditions; the model chooses no edge and cannot loop the run (tenet D11). The event run's transitions are archived before its record expires, and a proposal's accept or decline is an archived event keyed to the proposal, so a nudge's effect (the person joining the roster after that event) is computable from the archive.
12. **It answers no one, so it does not take the conversation's turn lock** ([ADR-033](./033-a-conversation-answers-one-turn-at-a-time.md)). The router's `claimTurn` is bypassed for it as it already is for the join recap, triage and stop evaluation (`router-agent-handler.ts`), and as the coordinator's event entry does not take it: a person's question is never queued behind it. **Decided:** an `agent` step that opens a task in the room (example a) does not take the lock either. The task an event opens awaits nobody (`EVENT_TASK_MACHINES`), so no person's answer is pending while the step runs, and a person writing meanwhile is answered on their own turn, with the task in the room.

**Three worked examples.**

**(a) The ops room opens and the assistant takes a first look.** An alarm opens an `ops` conversation through its `openedBy` declaration, and `conversation-opened` starts the ops assistant's workflow. Until participant roles are built (SPEC-CONVERSATION-TYPES FR30), the lead is the person the opening event named, `event:person`; once they are, the same workflow names `participantRole:lead`.

```yaml
workflows:
  ops-room:
    on: conversation-opened
    types: [ops]
    initial: opening
    states:
      opening:                         # instant, platform: mentions the lead (decision 6)
        send: { to: room, template: ops-room-opening, mention: { lead: event:person } }
        onSuccess: greet-lead
      greet-lead:
        send: { to: { people: event:person }, template: ops-lead-welcome }
        onSuccess: first-look
      first-look:                      # agent step: one turn of the ops assistant
        agent:
          prompt: ops-first-look
          workflow: incident_first_look  # the runtime opens this task; the turn runs its first step
          tools: [read_deployment_health]
          to: room
          deadlineMs: 20000
          fallback: { send: { to: room, template: ops-first-look-unavailable } }
        onSuccess: done
        onFailure: done
      done: { disposition: success }
```

The runtime opens an `incident_first_look` task in the room (a machine whose initial step reads the deployment's health, built, `task-state-machines.ts`), and the turn runs that step: it reads the health on the ops assistant's own session and posts what it found. `guided_troubleshooting` is not this machine: its first state asks the requester a question, and a task opens on a person's turn (SPEC-TASK-STATE-TRANSITIONS FR21). The task then rests on its next step as ordinary work in the room. **Decided:** a task opened by an event has no requester, so it is owned by the assistant whose step opened it, visible to the room, and no step of its machine awaits a party: the first look rests on `findings_shared` as the room's work, where anyone in the room asks for another read or says it is resolved. **As built** (`openEventTask`, `event-task.ts`; the processor's `eventStep` branch): the task is opened once per step of a run (its id follows from the run's key and the step), the turn runs the first step's instructions after the step's own prompt, and once the turn posted the runtime takes that step's one declared transition, never on a fallback. Only a machine in `EVENT_TASK_MACHINES` may be named, by a step that posts to the room and is not narrowed, and a version that selects its task workflows must offer it.

**(b) An arriving attendee is briefed for their participant role.**

```yaml
workflows:
  meeting-arrival:
    on: meeting-joined
    types: [meeting]
    initial: announce
    states:
      announce:                        # instant, platform: mentions who joined
        send: { to: room, template: attendee-joined, mention: { who: event:person } }
        onSuccess:
          - { to: welcome-back, when: { event:rejoin: true } }
          - { to: brief }
      brief:                           # agent step: drafts this person's brief
        agent:
          prompt: { fromRole: brief, else: role-brief }
          to: joined
          deadlineMs: 15000
          fallback: { send: { to: joined, template: why-you-are-here } }
        onSuccess: done
        onFailure: done
      welcome-back:                    # agent step: what changed while they were away
        agent:
          prompt: what-changed-since-you-left
          to: joined
          deadlineMs: 15000
          fallback: { send: { to: joined, template: welcome-back-short } }
        onSuccess: done
        onFailure: done
      done: { disposition: success }
```

The brief is a targeted message to the person who joined, so it may use their participant role (`participant:role`), the reason their invitation recorded and any other fact about them (ADR-060, decision 7). Their participant role comes from the meeting conversation type's declarations (DESIGN-CONVERSATION-TYPES section 6.1). Each arrival is its own event run and its own turn, and a rejoin is its own run (decision 5); batching several arrivals into one fan-out waits on fan-out (rule 10).

**(c) Someone has not turned up, and the organizer is offered a nudge.**

```yaml
workflows:
  chase-absent:
    on: expected-attendee-absent
    types: [meeting]
    initial: draft-nudge
    states:
      draft-nudge:                     # agent step: drafts, and proposes rather than sends
        agent:
          prompt: draft-attendance-nudge
          propose: [send_nudge]        # reaches a person who is not in the room: consequence
          to: { participantRole: organizer }
          deadlineMs: 20000
          fallback: { send: { to: { participantRole: organizer }, template: attendees-not-joined, mention: { absent: event:absent } } }
        onSuccess: done
        onFailure: done
      done: { disposition: success }
```

The nudge goes to people who are not in the meeting, by email, so it is consequence and the organizer confirms it (FR11, rule 7). The step receives no conversation history, only `event:absent` and the meeting's declared fields (rule 6), and the proposal can be applied only by the organizer it names (rule 8). `invitation-unredeemed` runs the same shape addressed to the invitation's inviter (`{ people: invitation:inviter }`, ADR-060): built as the platform's `chase-unredeemed`, which a version copies. The chase of an absence is **NOT BUILT**: `expected-attendee-absent` waits on the meetings code.

## Decision 9: a welcome is one special workflow that `WelcomeIntent` starts

**The welcome is the assistant's workflow on `assistant-joined`, and what `WelcomeIntent` does is decided by that workflow's steps.** A version declares it in `eventWorkflows` like any other event workflow; there is no separate `welcome` field and no mode to choose. Its opening is one of three shapes:

| Shape | What greets | Model call | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A template step (`opening`) | The built-in opening over the step's orientation fields (`companyName`, `companyBlurb`, `accessBlurb`, `examples`, `platformNote`), or the step's own `text` with conversation fields (`renderWelcomeTemplate`); a step with no field of its own says the deployment's orientation | none: instant | Built (2026-10-29) and deployed to dev |
| The template step, then a prompt step (`prompt`) | The instant opening, then one model turn of the joining assistant from a prompt template, with read tools, under a deadline | one metered turn, after the opening | Built (2026-10-29) and deployed to dev |
| Steps that greet and then continue | The opening (a template step or a `send`), then any step: a greeting `send` to the subject, an intake (`start_intake`), an `agent` step | only in its `agent` and prompt steps, after the opening | Built (2026-10-29) and deployed to dev |

- **The room's conversation type selects the welcome.** Of the version's `assistant-joined` workflows, the one whose `types` name the room's type runs, else the one that names no type, else the platform's `welcome` (one opening step with no field of its own, so the classification's primary says the deployment's orientation and any other assistant the built-in opening). A version declares at most one welcome that names no type, and no type in two of them (`welcomeWorkflowOf`, `versionEventWorkflowsErrors`). A per-conversation-type welcome is a welcome workflow whose `types` name the type.
- **A room an event opens runs the workflow its type selects.** The `conversation-opened` workflow whose `types` name the room's type runs once its people are in it (`openedWorkflowOf`), and in that room the join posts nothing, so the room is greeted once (decision 5's table, `arrivalWelcomeOwner`). One workflow answers for a type.
- **How `WelcomeIntent` says it** (`welcomePlan`). A welcome whose first step is its opening and is followed by nothing but one prompt step is said by the intent's own reply: the composed opening, the intake question after it where an intake applies, or a drift-spawned room's answer in place, and its prompt turn dispatched behind it, as before. Any other welcome runs: the router posts each step, its opening step posted as the assistant with `turnKind: welcome` (`postWelcomeOpening`), the intake is the workflow's own step, and the intent returns the one message the channel flow denies (`NOTHING_TO_POST`), so the join leaves nothing of its own in the room.
- **The template step's renderer is the structured composition, word for word.** `selectOpening` reads the step's text and fields through the parser the deployment parameter goes through, and `composeOpening` renders them, as the retired selectors did. `backend/test/lib/a-welcome-is-a-workflow-word-for-word.test.ts` holds every migrated welcome to the retired selectors' output in every kind of room, every room context (fresh, a topic, a drift-spawned room, an event summary, a carried-over visit) and both sides of the deployment-orientation fallback.
- **The prompt step reuses the join recap's dispatch.** The router asks for the turn through `invokeAsync` (`dispatchWelcomePromptTurn`, `welcome-prompt-turn.ts`), which records the turn routing the session is vended from. The turn bypasses the turn lock, is metered `machineWritten`, declares the kind `welcome`, and passes the output guardrail and marker strip. Its tools are the ones the step names, read tools only (decision 8, rule 5). Past the deadline, on a failed or blocked call or a refused meter, nothing more is posted: the opening already in the room is the welcome. The correlation is the conversation and the assistant, so a redelivered `WelcomeIntent` collapses on the processor's claim. Nothing follows a prompt step in its workflow, because its turn posts after the run has moved on; validation refuses a step after it.
- **The welcome's own steps run on `assistant-joined` alone.** Validation refuses an opening or a prompt step on any other event, a workflow with two openings, an opening that is not the first step (it opens the room), and a prompt step as the first step of an arrival (decision 8, rule 1).
- **A stored welcome is carried, not read twice.** `npm run migrate-legacy-welcome` (`legacyWelcomePlan`, `legacy-welcome-migration.ts`; `--dry-run`, `--assistant`) reads each active version's `welcome` and writes the workflows that greet the same way as a new version through the profile API: a draft cloned from the active version, `welcome: null` and the planned `eventWorkflows` patched onto it, validated and activated. An assistant that already has a draft is skipped and named. A variant's type becomes its workflow's `types`, a named platform workflow is copied in, and a type a variant took is removed from every other welcome's `types`. What a welcome said that a workflow cannot say is reported: the orientation fields beside a workflow welcome, which opened only a drift-spawned room. Nothing on the runtime path reads a version's `welcome`, and a draft, validate, activate, an experiment's variant, an import and a rollback refuse a version that carries one, naming the script (`retiredWelcomeErrors`). A first draft copied from a version that still carries one copies its workflows.
- SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR22 to FR25 state the requirements.

## Options considered

- **A separate "message plan" construct of conditioned sends.** It needs its own rules for what the workflow already says by step order (which message goes first, whether the intake follows the greeting, that nobody is greeted twice) and a second editor. Replaced by this decision.
- **Keep `WelcomeConfig` on the conversation type and wire it (SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR20).** It puts an assistant's words on the room, which `welcome-orientation.ts` already records as the wrong owner, and leaves the meeting and workflow messages to be built again separately.
- **A welcome feature, then a separate meetings notifier and a separate step notifier.** Three implementations of one pattern, which is the per-feature growth ADR-062 removes.
- **An event run with no task record.** It would need its own exactly-once claim and its own log to show which greeting a person received; the task record already has both.
- **Start `assistant-joined` from the code that adds the assistant, instead of `WelcomeIntent`.** Every path that adds an assistant (creation, adding a role, the console, a direct API call) would have to name the event, and a path that forgot would leave a conversation with no welcome. The Amazon Chime SDK fires `WelcomeIntent` for all of them.
- **Greet from live membership at `WelcomeIntent`.** That read misses the creator in a measurable share of creations; the participant shape does not.
- **An audience that names people directly on the version.** A version could then message anyone in the deployment; a platform-trust variable bounded to members cannot.
- **A trigger per feature, such as a second target on the meeting lifecycle rule invoking the fan-out coordinator.** It works for one feature and gives each next feature its own exactly-once claim, its own routing to the right classification and its own record of what ran. Replaced by one event set every feature uses.
- **A periodic sweep for unredeemed invitations and absent attendees.** It re-derives on a timer what a schedule created with the invitation or the meeting answers once, which tenet D6 refuses as the primary mechanism.
- **Schedules created by each feature's own path** (the invitations function, the meeting apply endpoints). Each path would need a scheduler grant and would create a schedule whether or not any workflow declares the deadline event. The router holds the version and creates one only when a workflow needs it.
- **A conversation-scoped meter for event runs.** A new meter beside the join recap's `machineWritten` rule would be a second rule for the same kind of turn (tenet D9); the global budget and the structural bounds of decision 5 cover the volume.
- **Event workflows of platform steps only.** A template cannot summarize state it has never seen: an incident room's first look, a participant role's brief and a drafted nudge each need reasoning. Kept as the rule for the opening, which stays a platform step (decision 8, rule 1).
- **An `agent` step that acts on its own.** Its writes would come from model output with no person in the loop, which SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS FR11 refuses for anything consequential.
- **A denylist of consequential tools for the `agent` step.** Any state-changing tool not on the list would run unconfirmed; the registry's `changesState` declaration with an explicit `propose` list refuses by default.
- **A model-chosen edge in an event workflow.** It would let a run loop or wander, and decide from what the model wrote (tenet D11). The runtime takes an `agent` step's outcome edges.
- **A model-written welcome in place of the opening.** The room would wait on a model call on its highest-visibility surface (SPEC-WELCOME-AND-CONTEXT FR2). The `prompt` mode keeps an instant opening and adds the model-written welcome after it.
- **Fallback chains between welcomes.** A welcome that runs falling back to a prompt turn, then to the opening alone, would make what a room receives depend on which part failed. Each welcome carries its own instant opening, and a prompt or `agent` step its own fallback.
- **A welcome field beside the workflows, with a mode.** It was built first (the three welcome modes) and retired by the 2026-10-29 amendment: a mode picker and a workflow name were two ways to say which workflow greets, and a per-type variant inheriting the version's fields was a second selection rule beside a workflow's `types`.

## Consequences

- The welcome, the join recap, meetings' start and join messages, and a step's notices are one mechanism: a workflow, `send` steps, `agent` steps, named audiences and message templates.
- An author changes what a welcome says, to whom, in what order, and whether the intake runs, by choosing a mode and, in the `workflow` mode, editing a workflow, with no platform rule to learn.
- A host-created conversation's targeted sends may use `host:` facts; a `room` send may not (ADR-060, decision 7).
- Each send's template carries its own languages and voice (ADR-062, decision 4), so a welcome localizes with no separate feature.
- Every conversation the assistant joins writes one short-lived task record for its welcome run.
- A voice greeting, a `prompt` welcome and an `agent` step each cost one model call per run, counted by the global budget and on the ledger.
- Meetings, invitations and event-opened rooms trigger work through the same events, the same router and the same record of what ran, and a new occasion is a platform-declared event rather than a feature's own trigger.
- An `agent` step costs one metered model turn per run (one per leaf once fan-out exists), each a named, timed `event_step` turn on the ledger with its outcome, and its deadline bounds how long anyone waits for it.
- A room message can say who joined or who leads by name, and the facts about a person stay with that person.
- Each invitation, scheduled meeting and started meeting carries one one-shot schedule while it matters, and only when a workflow declares the deadline event.
- A person can stop reminders and nudges by kind, as they stop any non-transactional notice.

## What is not built

Each is blocked outside this ADR's build, for the reason it names.

- **Deferred until the meetings code lands on main:** `meeting-joined` with its arrival brief and rejoin edge, `meeting-started`, `meeting-starting` and `expected-attendee-absent`; the `meeting-reminder` and `meeting` notice kinds; the `create_meeting` and `invite_attendee` proposals; a schedule's `UpdateSchedule` grant, which only a moved meeting needs; the meeting lifecycle rule as a declared source in the router's resource policy; and the meeting ending as an incident-watch trigger. **NOT BUILT.**
- **Waiting on a vendor adapter:** the `resolveVia` participant-role source and the creator's connector grant of "IAM this adds", because no connector implements `lookupRole`; synthesis refuses the source with that reason. **NOT BUILT.**
- **Waiting on sub-agent identity**, which SPEC-ASSISTANT-MEETINGS ("What Blocks This, In Order", row 1) lists as BLOCKED: an `agent` step's fan-out (decision 8, rule 10), and so the incident watch's investigation rounds and the re-check after a round. Until it exists an `agent` step is one turn of the assistant, as built. **NOT BUILT.**
- **Waiting on a deploy of this branch:** a console e2e that creates an assistant in the creation wizard, writes its welcome on the Workflows step and activates it, because the wizard builds a real assistant before its greeting step opens. The Workflows step and the Welcome area are proven in unit (`ProfilesTab.assistants.test.tsx`, `ProfilesTab.fields.test.tsx`). **NOT BUILT.**
- **Waiting on a deploy of this branch:** the visual workflow builder's e2e, `tests/e2e/admin-workflow-builder.spec.ts` (a workflow made on the canvas, connected, dragged, saved, still in place after a reload, valid on the server and previewed; in `validate.mjs`'s `profiles` phase), written and not run. **NOT BUILT** (its run).
- **Waiting on a notify kind:** a room message emailed to named people (`{ room: true, email: { people } }`, decision 3), and so a participant role's holders as a room email's recipients. No step emails a room message, and the notification bridge declares no kind such an email is sent under for a person to mute (SPEC-NOTIFICATION-BRIDGE FR19). **NOT BUILT.**

An `agent` step addressed to an invitee is refused, by decision: the invitee reads nothing in the conversation until they redeem the invitation, so a draft reaches them only as the inviter's confirmed reminder (`send_nudge`, decision 8, rule 6).
