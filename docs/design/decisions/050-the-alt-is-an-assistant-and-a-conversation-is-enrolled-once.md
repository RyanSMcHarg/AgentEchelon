## The problem, in one example

An operator wants to find out whether a warmer persona answers better than the current one.

A variant that names a model, an image model and a prompt addendum can express "which model is best" and cannot express that. A variant may also carry a `profileRef` naming a whole profile version, which the runtime serves, but that names an artifact somebody has to author.

The second half of the problem is quieter. An experiment that resolves its eligibility on every turn can change what a person is served between two messages in one conversation.

## The decision

### The alt is an assistant, and its profile is its own

An alt is the alternate assistant of a `(classification, role)`, which ADR-048 makes an assistant like any other. So it has a profile of its own, under its own name.

**The version history under the alt's name is the record of what has been tested**, which is the argument DESIGN-SEPARATE-ASSISTANTS section 3.7 makes for this shape. The live assistant's history holds only versions that served or were promoted: the control is its active version, referenced by number, and nothing an experiment derives is published under the live name ([DESIGN-EXPERIMENTS-BATTLE-DECISION-LOOP](../../specs/interaction/quality/DESIGN-EXPERIMENTS-BATTLE-DECISION-LOOP.md) §3.5). A candidate carries `derivedFrom` naming its experiment and variant, and `activateExistingVersion` refuses it as a rollback target, naming the Promote action.

**Promotion within an instance is a hash-checked copy** ([DESIGN-EXPERIMENTS-BATTLE-DECISION-LOOP](../../specs/interaction/quality/DESIGN-EXPERIMENTS-BATTLE-DECISION-LOOP.md) §3.6, [SPEC-BATTLE](../../specs/interaction/quality/SPEC-BATTLE.md) FR12). The candidate's body is copied into the live assistant as a new version and activated, only when the live active version is still the experiment's control and the copy hashes to the candidate's `configId`, both checked before any write. The new version records `promotedFrom` (the candidate, its version, the experiment), and a turn it serves is attributed to the `configId` the experiment measured. **Export and import is the path between instances**: `exportManifest` emits an instance-independent manifest and `importManifest` lands it as a DRAFT under the target name, which an operator reviews and activates.

### It is created from the experiment, and edited there

The alt's profile is **derived from the experiment's settings**: the live assistant's active body at arm time, plus the experiment type's recipe, published under the alt's name.

**It is editable only from the experiment settings**, with one exception. For a `profile`-type experiment, which pits two whole assistant definitions against each other, the full profile is editable **except its intents**.

**That rule is enforced by the handler, not by the console.** An alt's profile is a profile like any other, so `POST /admin/profiles/draft` and `/activate` would accept an edit to it from anyone holding `manage-profiles`, and a rule the API does not enforce is a convention the first script breaks. `manage-profiles` therefore refuses the ordinary edit routes for a profile assigned as an `alt`, and the derivation at arm and the variant-version route are the way in. The console affordance follows the refusal rather than standing in for it. The taxonomy is not the experiment's to change, because a side that serves different use cases produces a score the other side cannot be compared against; `publishVariantVersion` refuses an `intentPack` in a candidate.

**The derivation pins its base.** It reads the live assistant's ACTIVE version at arm time and fixes the result, rather than tracking the label. Activating a new primary version mid-experiment would otherwise change the control's counterpart silently and make the measurement meaningless; an edit of an armed experiment keeps its pins, and a pause and resume pins both again.

### A conversation is enrolled once, at creation

**An experiment assigns its version when the conversation is created, and that assignment holds while the experiment is active (amended 2026-10-15).** That is every experiment armed with two pinned versions (`servesVersions`), any with a pinned version, a `profile` experiment, and any with a `profileRef` variant. A conversation is enrolled in the conversation-scoped experiment that was live for its classification at the instant it was created, or in none. Two recorded facts decide it: the conversation's `createdAt`, which every creating path stamps on its server-only context row, and the experiment's lifecycle, replayed from its append-only `transitions`. Because both are fixed, the decision does not depend on which turn evaluates it. The first turn that resolves it pins the arm on the same row as it stood at that moment, written only where absent: the experiment and variant ids, plus the version the arm serves (its `controlPin` or `altPin`, or a ref resolved to a concrete version). Activating a new version of the live assistant later does not change a conversation already running on its arm, and neither does a later edit to the experiment.

**The pin lives on the context row, not on the battle config row.** A battle config row's existence is what its readers take to mean a battle is enabled, so an enrollment-only row there would switch battles on. The context row is written by every creating path, read by the router on its own role, and readable or writable by no member. The proactive briefing creates through the same creator, so its conversation has a context row and is enrolled like any other `general` conversation.

**An ended experiment never keeps a conversation on a version (owner, 2026-10-15).** Only an ACTIVE experiment holds an enrolled conversation to its arm. When the experiment is completed (with any decision, `promoted_treatment` and `kept_control` included), deleted, or its end date passes, every enrolled conversation is served the live assistant's active version from its next turn: the winner after a promotion, the control otherwise. While the experiment is paused its conversations are also served the live active version, because an unjudged treatment must not keep serving; a pause releases them for good, so on resume only conversations created after the resume enroll. The reason is the customer: a losing version can be a worse experience, and holding a person on it after the comparison is over serves no measurement. The cost is accepted: a person whose conversation was on the treatment sees the assistant change mid-conversation when the experiment ends. A released conversation's later turns carry no `experimentId` or `variantId`, so only turns served while the experiment was active count. As built, `enrollmentHolds` in `experiment-manager.ts` checks on every turn that the experiment is active, inside its window, and has not left `active` since the conversation's `createdAt`, reading the experiment through the per-container cache, so a pause or an end reaches a conversation within one minute (`CACHE_TTL_MS`). A duel on an experiment that is no longer active ends through `endBattle` (`abandoned:experiment-ended`) instead of letting either side answer, and a new `/battle` is refused (`battleExperimentEnded`); pausing or ending already releases the alt and restores its name (`setAltAssignment`, `applyAltAlias`).

Which arm a conversation draws is deterministic: `assignVariant` hashes `channelArn:experimentId` with no assignment table. Asked per turn instead, an experiment would change under a person three ways: one armed mid-conversation starts applying at the next turn, one that ends reverts at the next turn, and an `intent`-type experiment matches only on matching turns. Enrollment closes the first and the third; the second is the amended rule above, by decision.

**An `intent` experiment serves a whole version to every turn of an enrolled conversation (amended 2026-10-15).** Its candidate differs from the live version in one route, `models.byIntent[<intent>].primary`, so turns of every other intent answer exactly as the control's do and no person sees the assistant change; which turns the experiment COUNTS is still its intent's (`countsTowardExperiment`). **A referenced profile is still refused on an `intent`-type experiment** (`PROFILE_REF_ON_INTENT_EXPERIMENT`): a referenced version may differ in anything, which is a `profile` experiment, not an intent one.

**One alt profile, N experiments, and no conflict, because the pin is a version.** A `(classification, role)` has one alt, so two experiments running on one classification derive into the same profile. They do not collide: a version is immutable once published, and a variant pins `name:N`, so an experiment armed later publishes a new version without disturbing what an earlier one is measuring against. The alt's `active` label is the only shared thing, and neither an experiment nor a duel needs it.

**An enrolled conversation keeps its assignment when the experiment ends or its treatment is promoted.** The alternative reverts mid-conversation, which is the thing this decision exists to prevent; a conversation that finishes on the version it started with is the coherent end state, and the experiment simply stops enrolling new ones.

## What this costs, stated once

**The treatment is published under the alt's name, and nothing under the live assistant's.** `publishVariantVersion` takes a base and a target and refuses any target but the alt of the base's own role, and keeps its `intentPack` refusal and validation. Arming derives the candidate and pins the control through `deriveExperimentVersions`, which reads the treatment's recipe from the experiment row, so neither the console nor an API caller restates a variant as a patch. `derivedFrom` is provenance on each candidate (`baseProfile`, `baseVersion`, the experiment and the variant).

**An experiment compares two variants in one classification.** Two versions of one assistant is the comparison, and an assistant acts in one classification (ADR-042), so arming refuses any other count (`EXPERIMENT_TWO_VERSIONS`, `EXPERIMENT_ONE_CLASSIFICATION`).

**Enrollment is one read per turn and one write per conversation.** The router reads the context row; an unpinned conversation with a `createdAt` is decided against the cached list of conversation-scoped experiments (active, paused and completed), and the pin is written once. An enrolled conversation serves its arm on every turn, the greeting included, ahead of anything armed since; a deleted experiment releases its pins. Enrollment samples only conversations created while the experiment runs, so an experiment accrues more slowly than assignment on every turn would.

**A row armed before the amendment keeps what it was armed with until it is paused and resumed.** A battle armed with a control derived under the live name keeps reading that version; an `intent` or `base_model` row with no pins keeps resolving its variant's model per turn. Resuming pins both versions under this ADR.

**A candidate per arm is bounded by reuse.** One version per `(experiment, variant)`, republished only when the derived body's `configId` differs from the pin the variant already carries.

## What this does not decide

**Whether the alt's `active` label is used at all.** A battle resolves the alt through the assignment record and an experiment through its pins, so the label may be redundant for both. Leaving it unused is safe; deciding it is not this ADR's.

**What the Experiments tab looks like** when it becomes the alt editor, including how it presents a full-profile edit with intents held back.

**Whether an experiment may enroll a conversation that already exists.** Enrollment at creation is decided; whether an operator may opt an in-flight conversation in deliberately is a separate affordance.
