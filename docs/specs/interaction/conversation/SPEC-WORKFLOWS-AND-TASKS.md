## Overview

A **trigger** starts a **workflow**, and a workflow's steps may create **tasks**. A trigger is one of four kinds: a platform event (an assistant joins, a conversation opens, a person joins, an invitation is sent or goes unredeemed, an alarm changes state, a task reopens, and, with the `meetings` capability, a meeting starting or ending); a person's message, through the intent the classifier labels it with; an explicit command a person invokes, a button in the client or a slash command; or an inbound fact from another tool. The command, the inbound fact and `task-reopened` are **NOT BUILT**; of the meeting events, `meeting-started` and `meeting-ended` are built. A workflow is a sequence of steps: send a message, run a model turn, run the intake, create a task, wait, end. A task is the tracked piece of work a person sees in their open work and on a card; it runs its own steps, as a state machine, and only those steps write its state.

Every trigger, run and task acts in one classification, the axis IAM evaluates for every principal: each actor's credential names only itself (bearer pinning), every new boundary ships with a test that is refused rather than a review, and each question has one store - Amazon Chime SDK Messaging for the conversation as it is now, the archive for the record of every turn, transition and edition, the Aurora projection for analytics, the task row for a task's state and the run record only for where a run stopped.

Terms, each defined once: the **run level** is a workflow's own steps, taken by the router's runner; the **task level** is a task's machine, written by the task service. An **intent workflow** is a workflow whose trigger is an intent. A **create-task step** is the run-level `task:` step. An **indirect worker** is an indirect participant who works a task. A **settlement** is a task reaching the ending a run waits on; its **naming** is the task service's notice that continues the run. A **split** opens a new conversation and leaves the subject in this one; a **move** takes it out of this one.

Two kinds of participant take part. **Direct** participants are members of the conversation: people (`AppInstanceUser`) and assistants (`AppInstanceBot`), over chat, or over a text or a call through the deployment's number. **Indirect** participants never join: a service, or a person working in another tool, moves the workflow by an event to the platform and receives work by an event from it. The platform does not decide who does the work; the deployment's own tools do, and the platform adds the person they name.

## Business Problem

Multi-step work in a conversation needs a definition a team can read and edit, a record of who owes the next step, and a way to reach the people and systems that do the work. Without one model, an event's steps and a request's steps are two engines edited in two places, and a team cannot tell which one runs or what state it is in. This spec states that model once - triggers, workflows, tasks and participants - and records every requirement's status in one table, so nobody has to read six decision records to learn what is built.

Personas are defined in [PERSONAS.md](../../../overview/PERSONAS.md).

### End user

**Goal:** ask for something that takes several steps, see who owes the next one, and answer it where they already are.

**Goal:** define what an assistant does when something happens or someone asks, edit it per version, and measure each step.

| Compare one edition of a workflow with the next | WT60, WT109 |
| See how often work goes back for rework | WT105 |

**Goal:** know what is running, who holds it, and when it stalls, without reading code.

| Use case | Requirement |

| Trust that a team's edit cannot reach another classification | WT63, WT117 |

### Platform developer (wiring the deployment's other systems)

**Goal:** let the field-service tool, the ticketing system, the pager or an internal app move a workflow, and add the people they name, without the platform replacing them.

## Functional Requirements and Acceptance Criteria

**Priority is a keep-or-cut decision set against what the field expects.** `Table stakes` means a deployment without it is behind. `Differentiator` is worth the cost because it is uncommon in the field. `Gap - prioritize` marks a requirement whose absence puts this below the field's expectation. `Above bar - optional` is more than the field asks. `Optional - attach instead` should be bought rather than built. `Enabler` is not user-visible and is what makes another row work. **Status** names the code and the proof: **Verified on dev <date>: <e2e spec>** where a deployed run passed; **Built** with the code and its unit tests where none has; **Partly** with what is and is not built; **Accepted** or **Proposed** for a decision not yet built, with **NOT BUILT** or **OPEN**. **Detail** is where the mechanism is described; it carries no status of its own.

## User Experience Description and Mocks

**A person asking.** A request that takes several steps opens a task: the assistant asks only for what the step needs, the work appears in the person's open work with what it needs, and an approval or a choice comes on a card they answer in place. After delivery, a report can be revised. A private request stays private: nothing about its steps reaches the room or another tool, and the person is told when a step was withheld (**NOT BUILT**).

**A person asked to act.** A workflow that needs someone's approval tells them privately, with the card, and the task is in their open work until they answer or its deadline passes. A technician added by phone gets texts and calls from the deployment's number, each text from a message template carrying the message or a link, and answers by reply (**NOT BUILT**). A reviewer in another tool answers there, and the decision returns to the conversation naming them (**NOT BUILT**).

**A team editing.** Under the assistant's Workflows, one list grouped by what starts each workflow, Welcome first; the welcome's Edit switches between a template, a prompt and a workflow, and a workflow opens in the builder; the platform's own workflows are a separate, read-only section with a note that they open, show, close and queue tasks. A multi-step intent's card links to its entry, where View steps opens its task steps on the builder.

```text
Workflows                                     (stage 1)
  Welcome
    general conversations ........ Template   [Edit: Template | Prompt | Workflow]
    ops rooms .................... Workflow   [Edit]
  When something happens
    Conversation opened  ops-room (rooms: ops) ......... Shipped  [Edit]
    Person joined        join recap (added by the platform, read-only)
    Alarm state changed  incident-watch ................ Shipped  [Edit]
  When someone asks
    Write a report       report_generation  gather > outline > draft > deliver   [Edit]
    Brief an account     account_brief      research > draft                      [Edit]
    Add to my plan       place_item (platform, read-only)
  Platform  - used for managing task opening, viewing, closing and managing queues
    approval  acknowledgement  action_item  place_item  queue_drain  queued_request ...
```

**An operator.** Effectiveness reads each step of each intent per model. It reads runs per workflow edition (WT109) and reopens per task by intent and machine edition (WT105), from the run columns, the run-end record (WT62) and the settlement records (WT61) the archive holds. The fact for an inbound event (WT61) is **NOT BUILT**: it lands with the connectors pass.
